## 1. Positioning

**Positioning statement.** For Hebrew-speaking learners aged 16 and over, from people who cannot yet read English
letters to students and professionals who must argue in writing, Lucid is a structured English course taught from
Hebrew. It starts at the alphabet with Hebrew explanations, withdraws Hebrew step by step as the learner's English
grows, and is designed to lead to C1-C2 argumentative writing. Its writing feedback asks learners to find and fix
their own mistakes before it shows a correction, and its progress screens report only what the learner has
demonstrated, labelled as a Lucid estimate aligned to CEFR descriptors, never as a certified level. Of the 17
products profiled in our research, none teaches English from Hebrew from zero (source: 01-market-research.md,
section 1, item 1), and none teaches C1-C2 argumentative writing as a skill with feedback on the argument (source:
01-market-research.md, section 1, item 3; A-competitors-discovered.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 1).

**Limits on using this statement.**

- It describes the intended product. In this build the path from zero is complete only at Pre-A1, B1-C2 is mostly
  unbuilt (snapshot above), and no learning outcome has been measured. It is internal direction, not public copy,
  until the proof points in section 5 exist.
- `PRODUCT.md` calls Lucid "the only learning path" of its kind. The research supports a narrower statement: none
  of the 17 products profiled and none of the Israeli long-tail apps found (source:
  A-competitors-discovered.md, Israeli long tail (discovered, not fully profiled)), from desk research without
  interviews (source: 01-market-research.md, section 4). Use "none of the products we reviewed", not "the only".

**Who Lucid is not for.**

| Not for | Why | Where they are better served (from the research) |
|---|---|---|
| Anyone under 16 | Lucid's audience is 16+ (`PRODUCT.md`). Onboarding refuses under-16s and the database rejects them | Not assessed |
| Learners who need a certificate or an exam score (IELTS, TOEFL, Bagrut, AMIRNET) | Lucid gives estimates only and predicts no exam scores (section 4). An AMIRNET practice module is a plan (source: C3-hebrew-l1.md, Implementation decisions, I3) and is not built | Cambridge (CEFR-validated scoring) and British Council (EnglishScore certificate) carry credentialing authority (source: A-competitors-input-writing-institutional.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 10) |
| Learners motivated mainly by games, streaks and competition | Lucid has none of these (section 4) | Duolingo's verdict in the matrix: "Unmatched habit engine" (source: 02-competitor-matrix.md, Duolingo) |
| Learners who want accent reduction or a phoneme-level pronunciation score | Lucid gives no accent score (section 4) | ELSA Speak, phoneme-level pronunciation analytics (source: 02-competitor-matrix.md, ELSA Speak) |
| Learners who mainly want live conversation with a person | Lucid has no human tutors | Cambly, Preply, Jerusalem Post LiteTalk (source: 02-competitor-matrix.md, Cambly, Preply, Jerusalem Post LiteTalk) |
| People who want a tool to fix text they must submit or send | Lucid's AI does not rewrite learner text (section 4) | Grammarly, feedback "wherever they write" (source: A-competitors-discovered.md, Grammarly) |
| Learners whose first language is not Hebrew | The scaffolding and contrast notes are written for Hebrew | Arabic- and Russian-speaking Israelis have L1 options in Duolingo, Busuu and Memrise (source: A-competitors-core-gamified.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 1) |
| For now: B1-C2 learners who want a complete course today | Most B1-C2 lessons and the writing-lab exercises are not built | — |

## 2. The gaps in the market

Each gap pairs what competitors lack (Team A, deliverables 1-2) with what users complain about (Team B,
deliverable 3). Two caveats apply to every review count below:

- The coded store sample is stratified by rating, so counts compare themes within and across apps. They are not
  prevalence among all users (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 2).
- Trustpilot is biased toward billing and service disputes, and companies that invite reviews score higher.
  TrustScores "must not be ranked as a quality league table" (source: B-web-reviews.md, section 2).

### G1. Nobody teaches English from Hebrew, and nobody starts below the alphabet for Hebrew speakers

- **Capability gap.** None of the 17 products profiled offers an English course with a Hebrew interface, Hebrew
  explanations and Hebrew-aware feedback from zero, although ELSA, Speak, Praktika, Rosetta Stone and others sell
  in Israel with shekel prices (source: 01-market-research.md, section 1, item 1). The gap is specific to Hebrew:
  Duolingo, Busuu and Memrise serve Arabic and Russian speakers (source: A-competitors-core-gamified.md,
  Cross-competitor observations, item 1). The nearest products each stop short:
  - Loora sends complete beginners elsewhere.
  - LiteTalk's beginner course never mentions the Latin alphabet.
  - The Hebrew-medium apps that exist are stale or tiny.
  (source: A-competitors-discovered.md, Cross-competitor observations, items 2-3.)
- **User evidence.** A Duolingo reviewer cannot read the English questions; a Babbel reviewer cannot learn because
  the app is not in Hebrew; two of ten Memrise Hebrew reviews are rated 1 star only because Hebrew cannot be the
  base language (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 6).
- **Caveat.** The Hebrew evidence is "small (Hebrew reviews are a minority, and some are old) but consistent"
  (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 6). Babbel's Hebrew reviews date mostly from 2017-2019 and Busuu's
  from 2019-2020 (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 2).

### G2. The top of the ladder and argumentative writing are unserved

- **Capability gap.**
  - No product in the matrix is rated "strong" for C1-C2.
  - For writing feedback, only Grammarly is rated "strong". Busuu and Cambridge Write & Improve are "moderate";
    all others are "weak" or have none (source: 02-competitor-matrix.md, Capability ratings).
  - Duolingo stops at B2, and Babbel's C1 is something to "work towards" (source: 01-market-research.md,
    section 1, item 2).
  - "None claims C2, and none offers argumentative or academic essay instruction" among the four mass-market apps
    (source: A-competitors-core-gamified.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 2).
  - In the speaking apps, "advanced" means fluency polish (source: A-competitors-speaking-ai.md, Cross-competitor
    observations, item 3).
  - Write & Improve works at word and sentence level and is weakest at C2 (source:
    A-competitors-input-writing-institutional.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 3).
  - Grammarly edits rather than teaches. A systematic review reports limits on coherence and argumentation (one
    reviewed study) and a risk of over-reliance (several studies) (source: A-competitors-discovered.md,
    Cross-competitor observations, item 1).
- **User evidence.** Content too shallow or mis-levelled for intermediate+ learners is a repeated Trustpilot
  pattern at 7 companies, mostly in 2-4 star reviews (source: B-web-reviews.md, section 4, P3). Advanced learners are
  under-served across the field (source: B-web-reviews.md, section 6, repeated pattern 3).
- **Caveat: demand for writing is not shown.** Writing practice is "almost absent from reviews", and "absence of
  complaints is not evidence of demand" (source: B-web-reviews.md, section 4, P9). Independent reviewers barely
  evaluate writing: "an evidence gap, not proof of a market gap" (source: B-web-reviews.md, section 6, repeated
  pattern 4). Only 6 of 210 coded Duolingo, Babbel and Busuu reviews are evidently from English learners (source:
  03-user-review-analysis.md, section 2).

### G3. Feedback learners cannot trust

- **Capability gap.** AI conversation is a commodity; "what is scarce is AI feedback that is calibrated, explained
  and trustworthy" (source: 01-market-research.md, section 1, item 4). Pronunciation scoring errs in opposite
  directions: ELSA leans strict and Speak is judged "overly lenient" (source: A-competitors-speaking-ai.md,
  Cross-competitor observations, item 4).
- **User evidence.**
  - Speech recognition is Rosetta Stone's largest complaint code (21 of 70 coded reviews) and appears for Speak
    (7 of 62).
  - ELSA reviewers report the same pronunciation scored "excellent" then "incorrect".
  - Coders noted praise after nonsense for Praktika and Loora.
  (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 5.)
  - Distrust of AI content and AI tutors is a repeated Trustpilot pattern at 6 companies (source:
    B-web-reviews.md, section 4, P4).
  - Speech recognition rejecting correct speech is a pattern at 4 companies (source: B-web-reviews.md, section 4,
    P5).
  - One academic review criticises ELSA's "reliance on the US English accent as the evaluation yardstick" (source:
    B-web-reviews.md, section 6, R5).

### G4. Feedback without explanation or memory

- **Capability gap.** Write & Improve "assesses rather than teaches" and does not explain why something is wrong
  (source: A-competitors-input-writing-institutional.md, Cambridge Write & Improve). In a small 2026 study
  (n = 31), learners called its feedback grammar-centred and "too 'bot or AI'" (source:
  A-competitors-input-writing-institutional.md, Cambridge Write & Improve, dimension 6).
- **User evidence.** Reviewers find no cumulative error tracking (Speak), no post-lesson summary (Praktika) and no
  memory of past chats (Praktika on Trustpilot) (source: B-web-reviews.md, section 6, repeated pattern 2). Busuu
  users report an A1-to-A2 jump (7 of 70 coded reviews on level mismatch), and a Duolingo reviewer says new lessons
  "assume you know words you were never taught" (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 5).

### G5. Engagement and monetisation mechanics that interrupt learning

- **Capability gap.** In a reader poll, 62% of 17,984 votes chose "I dislike it" for Duolingo's Energy system
  (source: A-competitors-core-gamified.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 5). Speak moved from no
  gamification in December 2024 to leagues and streak freezes, and ELSA uses XP leaderboards (source:
  A-competitors-speaking-ai.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 6). Team C2 found no peer-reviewed evidence for
  streaks (source: 04-methodology.md, section 1, "Motivation without manipulation").
- **User evidence.**
  - Paywalls inside the learning loop reach 10% of an app's coded reviews in 5 apps.
  - Busuu users report unskippable ads before lessons (12 of 70).
  (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 4, items 2 and 5.)
  - A Duolingo reviewer spends "more time clicking and opening chests ... than on the lessons itself" (source:
    03-user-review-analysis.md, section 5).
  - Losing a streak or progress triggers 1-star reviews at 5 companies (source: B-web-reviews.md, section 4, P6).
  - Gamification fatigue among adults is repeated but concentrated in Duolingo (source: B-web-reviews.md,
    section 4, P7).

### G6. Billing, support and onboarding that break trust

- **User evidence.**
  - Trial-to-annual conversions, hard cancellation and refused refunds appear at 10 of 12 companies on Trustpilot,
    the strongest signal in that source. In the stores, billing is Praktika's top complaint code (13 of 70).
  - Unreachable support repeats at 8 or more companies on Trustpilot, and in the stores for ELSA, Memrise and
    LingQ (8-10 coded reviews each).
  (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 4, items 3-4.)
  - 19 of 30 sampled reviews of the new British Council English app are about being unable to sign up or log in.
  - Memrise's top complaint code is removed features (11 of 70).
  (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 5.)
  - Purchased "lifetime" access not honoured is a pattern at 3 companies (source: B-web-reviews.md, section 4,
    P8).

### G7. Weak placement, undeclared accessibility, little offline use

- ELSA has no placement test, and only Rosetta Stone's enterprise product documents a placement test feeding a
  CEFR path (source: A-competitors-speaking-ai.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 5).
- None of the four mass-market App Store listings declares accessibility features, Busuu says it is not yet WCAG 2.1
  AA conformant, and "No competitor offers RTL Hebrew with screen-reader support" (source:
  A-competitors-core-gamified.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 6).
- Offline study is rare or undocumented (source: 01-market-research.md, section 1, item 8).

### What is not a gap

- **AI conversation.** Duolingo says a Video Call costs it under $0.01, and Babbel includes Babbel Speak at no
  extra cost; "Has an AI tutor" is no longer a differentiator (source: A-competitors-core-gamified.md,
  Cross-competitor observations, item 4).
- **Speaking practice.** AI-only speaking costs about ₪200-₪410 a year on Israel's App Store (Loora) (source:
  A-competitors-discovered.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 4).
- **Travel and social scenarios.** The space is crowded (Praktika's slang tutor, ELSA's Dating Coach, Speak's
  roleplays, Rosetta Stone's Chat Missions); "Hebrew-medium coaching and register explanations would"
  differentiate, scenarios alone would not (source: A-competitors-speaking-ai.md, Cross-competitor observations,
  item 11).

## 3. Differentiators

Eleven differentiators follow. Each lists the gap it answers, what Lucid does, the closest competitor approach and
how Lucid differs, a status per component, and how it could fail. File paths are relative to `lucid/`.

### D1. Hebrew from the first letter

- **Gap answered:** G1.
- **What Lucid does.**
  - Hebrew is the default interface language. `setUiLang` sets `lang` and `dir` on `<html>` (`js/core/i18n.js`).
  - The course opens with "The English alphabet" and "Letter sounds" (`pa1-01`, `pa1-02` in
    `content/curriculum.json`). They cover capital letters, which Hebrew lacks, and sounds Hebrew does not have.
  - Onboarding offers a start-from-zero choice that skips the placement test and opens `pa1-01`
    (`js/views/onboarding.js`).
  - The validator requires Hebrew text in lesson fields at low levels (`tools/validate-content.mjs`).
  - Lessons carry "Watch out (Hebrew speakers)" notes (`hebrew_pitfalls`, rendered by `js/views/lesson.js`).
- **Closest competitor approach.**
  - **Jerusalem Post LiteTalk** has a Hebrew website and an AI coach that "gives explanations in Hebrew when
    needed" (vendor claim). Alphabet and true-zero instruction are not stated, and a directory describes its
    beginner course as for "adults with basic English" (source: A-competitors-discovered.md, Jerusalem Post
    LiteTalk, dimensions 4 and 16).
  - **Prolog** gives Hebrew-medium video lessons, but it was last updated in 2017 and assumes "good basic
    knowledge" (source: A-competitors-discovered.md, Israeli long tail).
- **How Lucid differs.** Self-study and interactive rather than coaching or video. It starts before the learner
  can read English letters, and the Hebrew is in the lessons themselves.
- **Status.**

| Component | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Start from zero without a test, straight into the first lesson | Built and tested | e2e "3. A learner can start at absolute beginner level (no test, first lesson)" |
| Lessons with Hebrew explanations and corrective feedback | Built and tested | e2e "4-6. Lessons have real content, exercises give feedback, progress is saved across reloads" (uses `pa1-05`); validator 0 errors on 31 lesson files |
| Hebrew interface on every route | Built and tested for accessibility only | `tests/e2e/a11y.mjs` audits 22 routes in Hebrew UI (0 violations). No test or review checks the quality of the Hebrew |
| A continuous path from zero to A2 | Partial | Pre-A1 10/10, A1 5/15, A2 11/15 lesson files. A beginner who finishes Pre-A1 meets an A1 level with 10 lessons missing |

- **Risk or way it could fail.**
  - Competitors can add a base language. Duolingo already offers English from about 43 base languages (source:
    02-competitor-matrix.md, Duolingo), and LiteTalk already markets Hebrew explanations.
  - Usability: no zero-level Hebrew speaker has tried to use the app.
  - Quality: no native editor has reviewed the Hebrew text.

### D2. Hebrew that fades on schedule, with English content kept intact

- **Gap answered:** G1 and G7. L1 support helps beginners most, and its benefit shrinks with proficiency (moderate
  evidence). The fading schedule itself is inference only (source: C3-hebrew-l1.md, section 6, Strength of
  evidence).
- **What Lucid does.**
  - Hebrew support follows the course level (`SUPPORT_BY_LEVEL` in `js/core/i18n.js`): full at Pre-A1 and A1,
    partial at A2, minimal at B1 and B2, off at C1 and C2. Settings let the learner override it
    (`js/views/settings.js`, `hebrew_support`).
  - The AI prompts use the same four levels (`HEBREW_POLICY` in `supabase/functions/lucid-ai/prompts.ts`).
  - Each English learning string is rendered with `lang="en" dir="ltr"`, and Latin runs inside Hebrew text are
    wrapped in `<bdi dir="ltr">` at render time (`isolateLatin`). English content is never mirrored.
- **Closest competitor approach.**
  - **Loora's** Hebrew localisation is "a translation aid" (source: A-competitors-discovered.md, Cross-competitor
    observations, item 2).
  - ELSA's Bilingual Mode and Speak's bilingual instruction exist for other first languages but document no Hebrew
    (source: A-competitors-speaking-ai.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 1).
- **How Lucid differs.** Hebrew is withdrawn on a plan tied to level instead of being a translation button, and the
  RTL/LTR mix is handled in markup.
- **Status.**

| Component | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Isolation of English runs inside Hebrew text | Built and tested | `tests/unit/i18n.test.mjs` "Latin runs inside Hebrew are isolated; punctuation around them stays Hebrew" |
| Accessible RTL and LTR interface | Built and tested (automated rules only) | `tests/e2e/a11y.mjs`: 0 violations. No manual screen-reader test; Chromium only |
| Fading schedule by level, learner override | Partial | Code in `js/core/i18n.js` and `js/views/settings.js`; no test asserts the schedule. The comment above `SUPPORT_BY_LEVEL` describes a different schedule from the code |
| Measuring Hebrew reveals per session by level (C3 decision S1) | Not built | — |
| Cross-browser bidi suite, at least 20 fixtures in Chromium, WebKit and Gecko (C3 decision R6) | Not built | Current browser tests run in Chromium only |

- **Risk.** If Hebrew is withdrawn too early, completion may drop, and C3 says this must be tested (source:
  C3-hebrew-l1.md, Implementation decisions, S1). Translanguaging effects for adults are "weak for adults and for
  durability" (source: C3-hebrew-l1.md, section 6, Strength of evidence), so the fading design must not be marketed
  as proven.

### D3. Writing feedback that makes the learner do the fixing

- **Gap answered:** G2, G3 and G4.
- **What Lucid does.** Feedback comes in two layers.
  - **Automatic checks, no AI** (`js/core/writing-analyzer.js`).
    - Pattern checks for common learner errors, with Hebrew explanations.
    - Connector and argument-structure signals, and length and must-include checks.
    - The UI says these checks can miss mistakes and can flag correct English.
  - **AI feedback, when configured.**
    - The model must quote the learner's text. The server drops any error whose quote is not in the learner's
      text, or whose correction is much longer than the quoted span (`postProcess` in
      `supabase/functions/lucid-ai/schemas.ts`).
    - The prompt forbids rewriting and requires a hint that is "never the answer" (`prompts.ts`, `rubric.ts`).
    - In the editor, each error shows first its location (B1 and up) or its type (Pre-A1 to A2), then a clue,
      then the correction with an explanation (`js/views/essay.js`).
    - Each task allows at most two AI revision cycles after the first draft.
    - Versions are compared word by word (`js/core/diff.js`).
    - Feedback carries "AI feedback can be wrong" and a "Report a mistake" button.
- **Closest competitor approach.**
  - **Cambridge Write & Improve** flags errors only above 90% confidence, gives no correct answers so learners
    self-correct, and releases feedback gradually. It does not explain why something is wrong and documents no
    argument or coherence feedback (source: A-competitors-input-writing-institutional.md, Cambridge Write &
    Improve, dimensions 2 and 6).
  - **Grammarly** gives deep feedback on the learner's own writing but corrects rather than teaches (source:
    A-competitors-discovered.md, Grammarly).
- **How Lucid differs.** Unlike Write & Improve, Lucid explains why and eventually shows the fix. Unlike Grammarly,
  it never rewrites the text and holds the correction back behind hints.
- **Status.**

| Component | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic checks without AI | Built and tested | `tests/unit/writing-analyzer.test.mjs` "flags common learner errors", "does not flag correct English", "register checks only for formal levels; structure signals are detected", "length and must-include checks gate completion"; e2e "11-12" asserts the check catches "more better" |
| Versions and word-by-word comparison | Built and tested | `tests/unit/diff.test.mjs` (2 tests); e2e "11-12. Writing can be submitted, evaluated (automatic + mocked AI) and revised with version comparison" |
| AI feedback with minimal-edit enforcement | Built, tested with mocks only | `handler_test.ts` "minimal-edit enforcement drops errors whose quote is not in the learner text" (not run here); e2e "11-12" with a mocked `lucid-ai`: the correction stays hidden until the learner reaches the end of the hint ladder, and the second draft is sent as draft 2 |
| Attempt required before each hint (E section 9.5, rule 1) | Partial | Pressing the button repeatedly reveals the correction without any fix attempt. E-advanced-writing.md lists "No hint ladders that a learner can click straight through to the answer" under What not to build. The H0 "self-check" rung is not implemented |
| Help level logged per error (E section 9.5, rule 5) | Partial | A `hint-level` event is logged (`js/views/essay.js`); nothing reads it and no test covers it |
| "Report a mistake" on AI feedback | Partial | The report is stored as an event on the device. The human audit process is "operational, not code" (source: 04-methodology.md, section 3) and does not exist yet |
| AI-confirmed writing errors feeding recurring-mistake drills | Partial | `js/views/essay.js` records them as attempts with an error type; no test covers this path |

- **Risk.**
  - Feedback quality from a real model has never been measured (D4).
  - Learners may click straight to the correction while the attempt rule is missing.
  - The pattern checks are rough and can flag correct English.
  - Write & Improve is free and its scoring is validated (source: A-competitors-input-writing-institutional.md,
    Cambridge Write & Improve, dimension 8). Lucid's feedback has to earn its place against a free, credible tool.

### D4. A writing rubric that is bounded and open about its uncertainty

- **Gap answered:** G3, and G2 at the top levels.
- **What Lucid does** (`supabase/functions/lucid-ai/rubric.ts`, `schemas.ts`, `js/views/essay.js`).
  - Twelve categories are scored 0-5 relative to the writing stage's target. Categories that do not apply at a
    stage are marked N/A and never turned into 0.
  - A task-fulfilment gate: a partial answer caps the argument categories at 2 and lowers the level estimate by
    one; an off-topic answer caps every category at 1.
  - Scoring can be repeated up to 3 times, taking the median. A spread above one band marks the category low
    confidence, shown as "~".
  - The overall level is shown as a range, and only on the final draft.
  - The prompt says scores must not be inflated, strengths must quote the text, and IELTS or Cambridge scores
    must never be mentioned.
  - From stage 7, learners rate themselves before seeing the AI's bands.
- **Closest competitor approach.**
  - **Grammarly's AI Grader** estimates a grade against an uploaded rubric (vendor claim); it is free once a day
    with three pieces of feedback (source: A-competitors-discovered.md, Grammarly, dimensions 5-6).

### D6. Levels that come from evidence

- **Gap answered:** G4 (level cliffs) and G7 (weak placement). "Lucid needs transparent validation of any level
  estimates it shows" (source: A-competitors-input-writing-institutional.md, Cross-competitor observations,
  item 10). None of Cambly, Preply, Loora or LiteTalk shows learners a CEFR-anchored progress record (source:
  A-competitors-discovered.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 6).
- **What Lucid does.**
  - **Adaptive placement** (`js/core/placement.js`). Items come in blocks of four at one level, the test stops at
    28 items, and "I don't know" counts as not known, never as a guess. The zero path skips the test.
  - **Per-skill levels** (`skillLevels` in `js/core/adaptive.js`). A level counts as demonstrated at 75% or more
    over at least 6 items at that level, and is lowered below 50% over at least 8 items.
  - **Mastery** needs time-weighted accuracy of at least 85%, at least 6 attempts, and two different days.
  - **Level checks** are unassisted: no hints, no explanations until the end, no AI (`js/views/checkpoint.js`).
  - **Progress screen** (`js/views/progress.js`). It shows where each level came from, labels it "Lucid estimate,
    aligned to CEFR descriptors", and says that finishing the course does not promise a level. It reports recall
    on reviews against the target, and 30-day recall once there are at least 5 probe reviews.
- **Closest competitor approach.**
  - **ELSA** computes a profile level A1-C2 automatically from pronunciation, fluency, listening and grammar
    (source: 02-competitor-matrix.md, ELSA Speak). Its C2 level is computed from speech and listening signals
    (source: A-competitors-speaking-ai.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 3).
  - **Rosetta Stone enterprise** documents a placement test that feeds a CEFR path (source:
    A-competitors-speaking-ai.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 5).
  - **LiteTalk** offers a free CEFR-based placement check of about 5 minutes (vendor claim) (source:
    A-competitors-discovered.md, Jerusalem Post LiteTalk, dimension 3).
- **How Lucid differs.** One level per skill, each with its evidence and its source. Nothing is awarded for time
  spent or lessons clicked through.
- **Status.**

| Component | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Adaptive placement | Built and tested | `tests/unit/engine.test.mjs` "placement finds the boundary for learners of known ability", "placement tolerates an occasional slip (1 in 8)" (simulated learners); e2e "2. A new user can complete a placement assessment" |
| Per-skill levels and topic mastery | Built and tested | `engine.test.mjs` "skill levels come from evidence; placement is a starting point that evidence can move", "mastery needs accuracy, enough evidence and two different days" |

### D7. A memory and mistake engine

- **Gap answered:** G4. Reviewers report no tracking of recurring mistakes in Speak and no spaced repetition in
  Praktika (source: B-web-reviews.md, section 6, R2 and R3).
- **What Lucid does.**
  - **Scheduling.** FSRS through the vendored `ts-fsrs`, with retention targets of 0.85, 0.90 or 0.95
    (`js/core/srs.js`).
  - **Grading from behaviour, not self-rating.** Wrong = Again; correct after a hint or slowly = Hard; correct =
    Good; Easy only when fast and already passed twice.
  - **Form recall.** English-from-Hebrew cards unlock once meaning recall is stable (stability of at least 7).
  - **Workload governor.** 8 new words a day, 4 when reviews pile up.
  - **Recurring mistakes.** The same error type 3 times on at least 2 days within 30 days counts as recurring, with
    a trend. The daily plan then adds a targeted drill (`dailyPlan` in `js/core/adaptive.js`,
    `js/views/drill.js`).
- **Closest competitor approach.** Quizlet offers "excellent low-friction vocabulary drilling with research
  support" but is "a tool rather than a course" (source: 02-competitor-matrix.md, Quizlet). Duolingo is rated
  strong on personalisation (source: 02-competitor-matrix.md, Capability ratings).
- **How Lucid differs.** One learner model links vocabulary memory, grammar mistakes and the daily plan. A mistake
  has to recur across days before it drives practice.
- **Status.**

| Component | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| FSRS scheduling and grading from behaviour | Built and tested | `engine.test.mjs` "objective grading: wrong=Again, hint/slow=Hard, right=Good, Easy only when fast and proven", "FSRS cards schedule forward, lapse back, and unlock form recall at stability >= 7", "due cards come most-forgotten first; vocab cards are not duplicated; workload governor"; e2e "8. ## 5. Proof points still needed

No real learner has used Lucid, and the persona reviews in this project are simulated. Until the measurements
below exist, Lucid should not present any differentiator as a learner benefit in public. At most it can describe
features that are built. Where the research sets a design or threshold, it is quoted. Elsewhere the threshold must
be fixed before the study starts, not after.

| Claim we would want to make | What must be measured | Minimum design (source) |
|---|---|---|
| Hebrew speakers who cannot read English letters can start and keep going | Completion and comprehension of the Pre-A1 lessons, and delayed recall, by zero-level Hebrew speakers aged 16+ in moderated sessions | Threshold to be set before the study. The current Hebrew-beginner evidence is small (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 6) |
| Hebrew support fades as learners improve, without hurting them | Share of Hebrew reveals per session by level (expected to fall). Whether forcing English at B1+ hurts completion | C3 decision S1, "How to test" (source: C3-hebrew-l1.md, Implementation decisions, S1) |
| Writing feedback you can trust | Evaluator-human agreement on Hebrew-L1 essays. A monthly bias audit. ## 6. Open risks to the strategy

1. **The core promise is mostly unbuilt.** 31 of 75 lessons exist: none at B1, C1 or C2, and 5 at B2. The
   writing-lab exercises and assignment bank are not built, and the e2e check for argumentative assignments fails.
   D5, the reason a B2-C2 learner would choose Lucid, is today a framework and a set of prompts.
2. **AI feedback quality is unknown.** The research shows LLM scoring is weakest on content criteria (source:
   E-advanced-writing.md, section 8.6). There is no calibration set, scoring runs once by default, and the AI
   features have only been tested against mocks.
3. **Demand for writing is unproven.** Reviews barely mention writing (source: B-web-reviews.md, section 4, P9),
   and few reviewers of the general apps are English learners (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 2).
4. **Hebrew-specific evidence is thin.** Team C3 found no peer-reviewed study measuring Hebrew-L1 adults' English
   vowel production, th or w substitution, article or copula omission, or aspect errors. The predicted errors are
   typological predictions (source: C3-hebrew-l1.md, Method and Limitations notes at the top of the report). The Hebrew review evidence is small and
   partly old (source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 6). One lesson note already states a prediction as fact
   (section 4).
5. **The Hebrew gap can close.** Duolingo offers English from about 43 base languages (source:
   02-competitor-matrix.md, Duolingo). LiteTalk already markets a Hebrew-speaking AI coach (vendor claim), and
   Loora is Hebrew-localised (source: A-competitors-discovered.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 2). The
   defensible part is curriculum depth and writing pedagogy, not the interface language, and that depends on
   building the content.
6. **The build contradicts some of its own principles.** Each of these would be the kind of trust gap the research
   criticises in competitors if shipped:
   - The hint ladder can be clicked straight to the answer, against E section 9.5, rule 1.
   - Recognition-graded `speak` items and self-ratings count as speaking evidence, against the decision in
     source: 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 9.
   - The comment on `SUPPORT_BY_LEVEL` does not match the code.
   - The Help screen says a skill gets a level only for correct answers "on different days" (`js/views/help.js`).
     `skillLevels` does not check days; only topic mastery does.
   - Settings say the correction setting applies to "conversations and the tutor", but roleplay requests do not
     carry it (`schemas.ts`; the roleplay prompt never corrects during a scene).
   - `content/manifest.json` lagged behind the lesson files (25 listed, 31 on disk), so the Path screen can hide
     built lessons until the manifest is regenerated.
   - The matrix's Lucid row claims full offline lessons and C1-C2 content that this build does not have.
7. **Cost and price.**
   - Writing evaluation is configured for a large model at high effort with up to three runs (`handler.ts`,
     `index.ts`), while competitors run AI conversation for under $0.01 a call (source:
     A-competitors-core-gamified.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 4).
   - There is no pricing, and billing is the strongest complaint theme in the market (G6).
8. **Credibility of a newcomer's levels.** Cambridge and the British Council have brand authority that a new
   entrant lacks (source: A-competitors-input-writing-institutional.md, Cross-competitor observations, item 10).
   Lucid's estimates may be ignored, or misread as certificates.
9. **Retention without streaks is untested.** The research supports removing manipulative mechanics but offers no
   evidence that a weekly goal alone keeps learners studying.
10. **Speech depends on the browser.** Recognition availability and quality vary, and Hebrew-accented English is
    not validated (`js/core/speech.js`; `PRODUCT.md`, Capabilities and Constraints).
11. **Data loss and sync conflicts.** Device-only data is lost if browser storage is cleared before an export. Sync
    resolves conflicts by keeping the newest write.
12. **The research has limits.** It is desk research only, with no interviews or usability tests with Israeli
    learners. Some features are known only from vendor material, prices and features change quickly, and Reddit
    was not accessible (source: 01-market-research.md, section 4; 03-user-review-analysis.md, section 2;
    B-web-reviews.md, section 2).
