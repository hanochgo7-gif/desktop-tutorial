# Team A — Competitor profiles: Speaking-first and AI-tutor apps (ELSA Speak, Speak, Praktika, Rosetta Stone)

> **Method.** Desk research run on **2026-10-30**. Each competitor was profiled across 16 dimensions: the 15 standard ones plus English taught from Hebrew. Every factual claim cites a page that was opened in this session with a fetch tool. Search-result snippets were used only to find pages and are never cited. Source types used: official help-center articles, vendor marketing pages, US and **Israel (IL)** App Store listings, one academic software review (CALL-EJ, 2021), press (TechCrunch), a vendor-published accessibility conformance report (VPAT), Trustpilot user-review pages, and third-party reviews. Several of the "independent" reviews are published by **competing products** (SpeakShark, LanguaTalk, Headway, LingQ, FluentU). Each one is flagged, and their evaluative claims are treated as indicative only. Vendor marketing claims are marked **(vendor claim)**.
>
> **Limitations.**
> 1. No app was installed or used hands-on, so UI, feel, and feedback quality come from documentation and third-party reviews.
> 2. Google Play listings could not be fetched. One academic page (Iowa State) was blocked by bot protection. Reddit was not used.
> 3. The fetch tool summarises pages with a small model. Exact numbers were cross-checked where possible, but some wording comes from that summariser rather than verbatim quotes.
> 4. App Store prices were read from the **US** and **Israel** storefronts on 2026-10-30. Stores list many SKUs without a tier label, so some prices could not be mapped to a plan. Prices change often and vary by region and promotion.
> 5. App Store release notes give dates without a year. Where a version is listed as "1d ago", the date seen is 2026-10-30.
> 6. Trustpilot profiles for Speak (2 reviews) and ELSA (33 reviews) are too small to be representative.
> 7. Rosetta Stone runs separate consumer (new "Sapphire" app, and a legacy "Classic" app), enterprise and school products. The CEFR-mapped content found is mostly from **enterprise** and **school** documents. Whether consumers can get it is **not verified**.

**What they do better than anyone (in this group).** Diagnostic pronunciation feedback. ELSA marks each sound right or wrong at the phoneme level, gives articulation tips, measures intonation in Hz, and shows timestamps for disfluencies [9][11]. Its Speech Analyzer also estimates IELTS, CEFR and TOEIC scores and lists grammar errors from free speech [11]. No other app here documents feedback this granular.

**Where they fall short for a Hebrew-speaking adult aiming at C1–C2 writing.**
- **No writing at all** beyond typed roleplay turns [10].
- **No Hebrew anywhere:** not in the UI, translations or Bilingual Mode [6][7]. Hebrew-specific pronunciation patterns cannot be targeted through the "Mother Tongue" setting either.
- **No placement test** [12].
- **"C2" means oral only.** The Profile CEFR level is computed from pronunciation, fluency, listening and grammar, so it is not evidence of C1–C2 literacy [5].
- **Billing trust problems** recur in reviews [19].

- **No documented Hebrew explanations** [40][41].
- **No CEFR scaffolding** [45].
- **No writing module** [43].
- **Feedback is hidden per message**, with no summary to learn from [49].
- **Memory and recognition reliability** draw repeated complaints [50].
- **Advanced learners plateau** [49].

## Cross-competitor observations

1. **Hebrew speakers are excluded from L1 support everywhere, yet all four sell in Israel.**
   - The L1 scaffolding on offer: ELSA's Bilingual Mode and translations (12–14 languages) [6][7], Speak's bilingual video instruction (15 L1s) [24], and Praktika's native-language toggle (L1 list unpublished) [40]. Rosetta Stone gives no one L1 support by design [63].
   - None documents Hebrew [6][7][24][41][58].
   - All four list ₪ prices on the Israel App Store [20][23][38][54].
   - IL rating counts give a rough proxy for local reach: **Praktika 5.7K, ELSA 1.7K, Rosetta Stone 565, Speak 48** [38][20][54][23]. Praktika appears to be the most-used of these in Israel, though rating counts are only a proxy.
2. **C1–C2 writing is untouched white space.**
   - ELSA, Speak and Praktika have no writing module [10][21][43].
   - Rosetta Stone's writing is dictation, type-the-answer, or vendor-claimed chat guidance [55][60][61].
   - None of the sources opened describes essays, argumentation, rubric-based scoring, or IELTS/TOEFL *writing* tasks.
   - The most "advanced" tool in the group, ELSA's Speech Analyzer, is entirely oral [11].
3. **"Advanced" means fluency polish, not proficiency.**
   - ELSA's C2 profile level is computed from speech and listening signals [5].
   - Praktika's advanced use is "reduce filler words" [44].
   - Speak sends advanced learners to free talk [26].
   - Only Rosetta Stone *enterprise* publishes B1–C1 content [62].
4. **Pronunciation scoring is calibrated badly in opposite directions.**
   - ELSA is judged too strict, and even penalised a native speaker [17][19].
   - Speak is judged too lenient, giving perfect scores to wrong output [33].
   - Praktika and Rosetta draw recognition complaints [50][66].
   - Calibrated, explainable feedback tuned to a known L1 (Hebrew) is an opening.
5. **Placement is weak.** ELSA has no placement test and uses self-selection [12]. Speak and Praktika publish no CEFR placement [26][45]. Only Rosetta Stone enterprise places learners on a CEFR-referenced path [61].
6. **Gamification is converging on Duolingo patterns, and it creates support load.**
   - Speak moved from "no gamification" (Dec 2024) [32] to 10-tier leagues and streak freezes [28][29].
   - ELSA uses XP leaderboards [14].
   - Praktika uses streaks and challenges [37].
   - Lost streaks show up as Trustpilot grievances for ELSA [19] and Praktika [50].
   - Rosetta Stone is the outlier with minimal gamification [64].
7. **Trust and billing are the dominant negative theme.**
   - Trial auto-conversion and refund disputes recur for ELSA [19] and Praktika [49][50].
   - Rosetta Stone has lifetime-licence lock-outs [66].
   - SKU sprawl (10+ SKUs per store) makes true prices opaque [1][37][54].
8. **AI is tier-gated.** ELSA's gen-AI starts at Premium [3]. Speak's unlimited personalisation needs Premium Plus [25]. Praktika's tutor memory needs Premium [39].
9. **Accessibility is barely declared.** None of the four declares accessibility features on the App Store [1][22][37][53]. Only Rosetta Stone publishes a WCAG 2.2 conformance report, and it exempts core learning images and listening captions by design [63].
10. **Offline is weak or undocumented.** Praktika needs internet, though lessons saved while online can be practised offline [46]. ELSA and Speak are undocumented [13][31]. Only Rosetta Stone Classic advertises full lesson downloads [53].
11. **The Real-Life English space is crowded.**
    - Praktika's slang tutor [47]
    - ELSA's travel and dating coaches [2]
    - Speak's roleplays [33]
    - Rosetta Stone's travel Chat Missions [55]
    
    Lucid's practical track will not differentiate on scenarios alone. Hebrew-medium coaching and register explanations would.
