# Team A — Competitor profiles: Input, flashcards, automated writing feedback & institutional (LingQ, Quizlet, Cambridge Write & Improve, British Council LearnEnglish ecosystem)

> **Method.** Desk research carried out on 2026-10-30. Every claim below cites a page that was opened with WebFetch during this session; search-result snippets alone were not used as evidence. Sources are vendor sites and help centres, Apple App Store listings (US and Israel storefronts), independent and competitor-authored reviews, press coverage, Trustpilot, and peer-reviewed or conference papers. Vendor marketing claims are marked **(vendor claim)**. Reviews written by competing products (StoryLearning, Lingopie, Nibble, My Engineering Buddy) are flagged, because they have a commercial reason to be negative. Prices are as seen on 2026-10-30, with currency and storefront.
>
> **Limitations.** No accounts were created and no product was tested hands-on, so every finding comes from documentation, listings and third-party reports. quizlet.com and help.quizlet.com returned HTTP 403, so Quizlet's official pricing and feature-tier pages could **not** be verified. The Quizlet price figures come from app stores and third parties. The British Council Israel site, the British Council accessibility statement, and the Cambridge accessibility statement returned 503, 404 or 403 errors. The LingQ help centre returned 404, and the AppleVis forum thread on LingQ accessibility returned 403. Google Play listings could not be read. Many Write & Improve help articles date from 2017 to 2022 and may not reflect later model updates. App Store "what's new" entries show no year. WebFetch returns model-summarised page text, so wording is paraphrased and small extraction errors are possible. Reddit was not consulted. About 29 fetch attempts failed and are not cited.

## LingQ

| # | Dimension | Findings | Evidence type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Target audience | Built for self-directed learners of 50+ languages (53 per the site) who want to learn from authentic content (vendor claim) [1]. Independent and competitor reviews agree that it fits learners at roughly lower-intermediate level and above who already have material they want to read or hear. They say it does not suit beginners who want guidance or learners whose focus is speaking [8][9][10]. The iOS app is rated 13+ [7]. | Official, independent review, competitor review, app store |
| 2 | Learning methodology | Comprehensible input through reading and listening. Learners tap unknown words to save them as "LingQs" with their context. Saved words move through four stages, from New to Learned, and words can also be marked Known directly. Review uses spaced repetition with multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank and dictation items, plus a daily email of due words [8]. LingQ stresses "encountering words naturally in meaningful contexts" and says it "isn't about repeating a small number of strange phrases or playing games" (vendor claim) [1]. Mini Stories repeat high-frequency patterns with native audio, a retelling from another point of view, and comprehension questions [4]. | Competitor review, vendor blog, official |
| 3 | Curriculum structure / CEFR | **No CEFR mapping** on the English page. Instead of fixed courses there is a topic library (Books, Podcasts, News and so on) plus imported content [3]. Lessons are labelled from Beginner 1 to "Advanced 2" [8]; Lingopie counts six levels [9]. There are 60 Mini Stories per language [4]. Category counts include News 20,000+ and Entertainment 10,000+ lessons (vendor claim) [1]. **CEFR range claimed: none.** | Official, competitor reviews |
| 4 | Beginner experience | **Not a true zero start.** Reviewers put the entry point at lower intermediate [8], or at upper A2 to lower B1, and report no onboarding guide or suggested path [9]. LingQ's own blog says Duolingo is "better for absolute beginners who need structure" [5]. No alphabet or script instruction was found (not verified). L1 support is a tap-to-translate dictionary whose language can be changed, but a 2022 US App Store reviewer reported still "constantly" getting English translations after switching dictionaries and the translation language [6]. The quality of Hebrew glosses was not verified. The UI is not available in Hebrew [1][7]. | Competitor review, vendor blog, app store |
| 5 | Advanced (C1–C2) | Strongest of this group for **receptive** C1–C2 work. Learners can import any article, YouTube video, podcast or ebook, and AI transcription turns audio into lessons [1][6][8]. There is **no productive C1–C2 path**: no structured grammar and no speaking practice [8]. The "Known words" count is inflated by default settings [8]. Some content is shared by members, so its quality varies [8]. | Official, app store, competitor review |
| 6 | Writing exercises & feedback | The **Writing Exchange** offers "real corrections from native speakers" (vendor claim) [6]. Writing correction is a tutored service paid for with points, separately from the subscription [2][8]. Lingopie says writing exchange is limited to Premium Plus [9]; that tiering is not confirmed by the official page [2]. There is no writing curriculum and no automated writing feedback was found. The Lynx chatbot gives "detailed tutor responses" (vendor claim) [6]. Whether it corrects learner writing was **not verified**. | Official, app store, competitor review |
| 7 | Speaking & pronunciation | No structured speaking practice and no pronunciation feedback [8][9]. Paid one-to-one tutor sessions of 30–90 minutes are bought with points [8]. AI text-to-speech voices are available [6]. LingQ's own blog calls it "weak for speaking practice" [5]. | Competitor reviews, vendor blog, app store |
| 8 | AI integration | Lynx AI assistant and chatbot (with web search), AI audio transcription, AI voices and AI-Simplified Lessons [6][7]. **Tiering:** Premium Plus adds 6× audio transcription, AI voices, Advanced Lynx Chat and AI-Simplified Lessons [2]. AI-Simplified Lessons are Plus-only [8]. | Official, app store, competitor review |
| 9 | Personalization / adaptivity | Learners pick their own content. LingQ claims it "always finds you content just above your level" (vendor claim) [3] and that "the LingQ system adapts to your skill level" (vendor claim) [1]. In practice, personalization means curating your own material. One reviewer notes that the library's openness can be tiring [8]. | Official, competitor review |
| 10 | UI & usability | The reader is colour-coded: unknown words in blue, saved words in yellow [8]. Other reviewers describe the interface as "outdated and cluttered", with a crowded mobile view and an "especially cumbersome" upload process [9], or as "dated" [10]. LingQ's own blog says the learning curve is "the most consistent criticism across reviews and user feedback" [5]. Ratings: 4.7 from 10K ratings on the US App Store [6]; 4.7 from 193 ratings on the Israel App Store [7]; 4.5 on Google Play from 50.6K reviews, as reported by StoryLearning [8]. | Competitor reviews, vendor blog, app store |
| 11 | Subscription & pricing | **lingq.com (USD, seen 2026-10-30):** The free plan allows 20 saved LingQs, 5 imported lessons and 1 playlist. Premium costs $14.99 a month, $119.99 a year or $215.76 for 24 months. Premium Plus costs $29.99 a month or $269.99 a year [2]. **Israel App Store (ILS, seen 2026-10-30):** Premium ₪54.00 a month, ₪290.00 for 6 months or ₪429.90 for 12 months; Premium Plus ₪107.90 a month or ₪969.90 a year [7]. **US App Store (USD, seen 2026-10-30):** Premium $14.99 monthly, $80.99 for 6 months, $119.99 for 12 months; Premium Plus $29.99 monthly or $269.99 yearly; plus unlabelled "LingQ Premium" SKUs at $109.99 and $99.99 [6]. The Israel listing also has an unlabelled "LingQ Premium" SKU at ₪394.90 [7]. StoryLearning (Sept 2026) reports UK prices of £12.99 a month and £103.99 a year, a 30-day refund for new Premium members, and that content is deleted when Premium is cancelled [8]. One review mentions a "lifetime" plan at about $200 [10]. That plan is **not** on the official pricing page [2], so it is unverified. | Official, app store, reviews |
| 12 | Strengths & weaknesses | **Strengths:** imports from almost any source; 50+ languages on one subscription; offline study; a large library [1][8]. **Weaknesses:** learning curve; a review queue that grows out of control; no grammar or speaking structure; uneven community content; a dated UI [5][8][9][10]. | Mixed |
| 13 | Retention mechanisms | Coins, a daily goal and streak rewards. Statistics and challenges are Premium features [8]. There are also streaks, rankings, word, book and 90-day challenges, and community forums [9]. LingQ lists streak tracking as a Premium feature [5]. **How it feels:** a 2025 US App Store reviewer found the inactivity reminders too strong (quoting one that said "this is your last message") and called them silly; the developer pointed to notification settings [6]. The review backlog builds up: LingQ's own blog warns "The SRS review queue can spiral" [5][8]. | Competitor reviews, vendor blog, app store |
| 14 | Accessibility | The developer declares no accessibility features on the App Store [7]. The homepage makes no accessibility claims [1]. Screen-reader and dyslexia support: **not verified** (the AppleVis forum thread returned HTTP 403). | App store, official |
| 15 | Offline | The apps advertise "Study offline and sync your progress automatically" [6][7]. StoryLearning places offline study in Premium [8]. The signup page lists offline access without saying which tier includes it [2]. | App store, official, competitor review |
| 16 | English course taught from Hebrew | **None.** Hebrew is offered only as a *target* language [1]. Neither the web interface (18 UI languages) [1] nor the iOS app [7] is available in Hebrew. No English course with Hebrew explanations was found. | Official, app store |

**What they do better than anyone.** LingQ has the best pipeline in this group for turning *any* authentic text, audio or video into a study unit, with tap-to-save vocabulary, transcripts and spaced repetition. For B2→C2 *receptive* growth (reading and listening volume), nothing else here comes close [1][6][8].

**Where they fall short for a Hebrew-speaking adult aiming at C1–C2 writing.** There is no Hebrew interface and no Hebrew-medium instruction [1][7]. It cannot take a learner from zero, including the Latin alphabet [5][9]. There is no grammar sequence and no instruction in argumentation. Writing feedback depends on native-speaker or tutor corrections paid with points, not on criteria-based feedback [2][6][8]. The learner must design their own path, and reviewers repeatedly name that as the main point of failure [5][8][9].

## Quizlet

| # | Dimension | Findings | Evidence type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Target audience | School and college students studying any subject, plus teachers. **It is not a language course.** Common Sense Media recommends it for ages 11+ [13]. The app is rated 4+ and contains ads and user-generated content [12]. Scale: over 60M monthly active users and 500M+ user-made sets (2021 figures) [19]. | Independent review, app store, encyclopedia |
| 2 | Learning methodology | Retrieval practice with flashcards: Flashcards, Learn (adaptive), Test (practice tests), the Charms puzzle game and spaced repetition [11][13], plus the free Match game [14][16]. Evidence base: a 2024 meta-analysis found a moderate effect on vocabulary achievement (Hedges' g = 0.62 across 21 studies) and on vocabulary retention (g = 0.74 across 5 studies), with high heterogeneity [21]. | App store, independent review, academic |
| 3 | Curriculum / CEFR | **No curriculum and no CEFR mapping.** Content is user-generated sets of varying quality [13][14]. **CEFR range claimed: n/a.** | Independent reviews |
| 4 | Beginner experience | No course, no alphabet instruction and no grammar sequence. A learner has to find or build sets. "Study sets can be created in any language" [14], so Hebrew–English sets are possible, but their quality depends on whoever made them. The UI is not available in Hebrew [12]. | Independent review, app store |
| 5 | Advanced (C1–C2) | It can drill advanced vocabulary but builds no C1–C2 skills. Common Sense Media says "It's trickier to study concepts and critical thinking subjects, but not impossible" [13]. The meta-analysis covers only vocabulary and attitude, not writing or speaking. It also cites a study in which paper flashcards beat Quizlet on delayed gains, especially for advanced learners [21]. | Independent review, academic |
| 6 | Writing exercises & feedback | No composition writing and no writing-feedback feature appears in the store listings [11][12] or the reviews consulted [13][14]. Anything beyond typed recall of set items is **not verified**. | App store, independent reviews |
| 7 | Speaking & pronunciation | Text-to-speech audio on some sets (tap the speaker icon) [13]. "Audio recording" and podcast features turn lectures into study material [11]. Speech recognition and pronunciation scoring were **not found or verified**. | Independent review, app store |
| 8 | AI integration | **Q-Chat**, an AI tutor built on OpenAI, launched in March 2023 [19] and was **retired after 30 June 2025** [17]. Current AI features: "Ask Quizlet" explanations and generating study sets from notes, uploads and lecture recordings [11]; Magic Notes [12]; AI study tools announced in February 2026 that move from explanation to practice [18]; the acquisition of Coconote, an AI note-taking app (February 2026); and a ChatGPT integration (March 2026) [19]. **Tier:** third parties list Magic Notes and AI features as Plus [14][15]. The official tier mapping is **not verified** because quizlet.com returned 403. | Independent, press, encyclopedia, competitor blog |
| 9 | Personalization / adaptivity | Learn is described as "Adaptive practice that meets you at your level and adjusts as you improve" (vendor claim) [11]. Common Sense Media says Learn, "based on performance, increases difficulty over time" [13]. Spaced repetition and "retention insights" are also listed [11]. | App store, independent review |
| 10 | UI & usability | App ratings: 4.8 from 1.1M ratings on the US App Store [11]; 4.6 from 16k ratings on the Israel App Store [12]. The web version shows an ad on each page [13]. Trustpilot reviewers complain about ads, pop-ups and constant upgrade prompts [20]. One Plus subscriber calls the app intuitive [20]. | App store, independent review, user reviews |
| 11 | Subscription & pricing | **Free:** ads, with a daily cap on Learn and Test rounds [14][15]. Learn and Test went behind the paywall on 1 Aug 2022. As of Aug 2024 the free plan allowed 5 Learn rounds and 1 practice test [16]. **US web prices (USD, third-party, Oct 2026):** Plus $7.99 a month or $35.99 a year [14][15]; Plus Unlimited $44.99 a year [15]. **Trial terms conflict:** My Engineering Buddy describes a 7-day free trial for student Plus without limiting it to annual billing [14]; Nibble says a trial is offered "only on annual plans" and gives no length [15]. **US App Store in-app purchases (USD, seen 2026-10-30, no billing periods shown):** Plus $9.99 and $44.99; Plus Unlimited $44.99; Teacher $35.99; Quizlet Go $44.99 [11]. **Israel App Store (ILS, seen 2026-10-30):** Plus ₪29.90 and ₪129.90; Teacher ₪105.90; Quizlet Go ₪129.90 [12]. The **official price page could not be opened** (HTTP 403). Common Sense Media's "$15 per year" [13] looks out of date. | App store, third-party reviews, press |
| 12 | Strengths & weaknesses | **Strengths:** a huge library of sets; fast vocabulary memorization with research support [21]; a polished, highly rated app [11]. **Weaknesses:** heavy paywall and ads, with a Trustpilot score of 1.4/5 from 662 reviews [20]; uneven set quality [13][14]; no language pedagogy, no productive skills, and no CEFR [13][21]. | Mixed |
| 13 | Retention mechanisms | The Charms puzzle game and Study With Friends [11]; the free Match game [14]; Quizlet Live, a team game launched in 2016 [19]. Daily streaks and badges: **not verified**. **How it feels:** upgrade prompts dominate negative feedback. On Trustpilot, 84% of reviews are 1-star, with complaints about paywalls, ads and billing after cancellation [20]. | App store, encyclopedia, user reviews |
| 14 | Accessibility | No accessibility features declared on the App Store [11][12]. A blind US App Store reviewer (Nov 2022) wrote: "If you use a screen reader, you likely already know Quizlet has never been accessible on any platform whatsoever." That is a single user's claim [11]. A VPAT or WCAG conformance statement was **not verified**. | App store |
| 15 | Offline | Reported as a Plus-only feature by a competitor blog [15]. Offline is not mentioned in the Israel listing [12]. Official confirmation is **not verified**. | Competitor blog, app store |
| 16 | English course taught from Hebrew | **None.** The 17 app UI languages do not include Hebrew [12]. There is no English course at all. Users can make their own Hebrew–English sets [14]. Whether Hebrew text-to-speech exists, and on which tier, was **not verified**. | App store, independent review |

**What they do better than anyone.** Low-friction, high-volume retrieval practice: making or finding a set and drilling it takes seconds. It also has the strongest research base in this group for vocabulary gains [21], and very high app-store satisfaction [11][12].

**Where they fall short for a Hebrew-speaking adult aiming at C1–C2 writing.** Quizlet is a tool, not a course. It has no sequence, no CEFR, no writing and no speaking feedback [13][21]. Its flagship conversational AI (Q-Chat) was withdrawn [17]. Monetisation through daily caps and ads hurts trust [16][20]. For Lucid, it is a model for how spaced-repetition vocabulary review *should feel*, and a warning about paywall backlash.

## Cambridge Write & Improve (writeandimprove.com)

| # | Dimension | Findings | Evidence type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Target audience | EFL learners who want writing practice without a teacher [36]. Teachers and organisations use +Class View, and IELTS and B2 First candidates use +Test Zone [22][25]. TESL Ontario lists ESL/EAL adults, secondary students and EAP learners [37]. | Official, help centre, press, professional association |
| 2 | Learning methodology | Write, get an instant automated assessment, revise and resubmit (a process-writing loop) [26][37]. Feedback is "indirect, semi-corrective": problems are flagged but **no correct answers are given**, so learners have to self-correct [26]. Feedback is released gradually so learners are not overwhelmed [26][27]. | Help centre, professional association |
| 3 | Curriculum / CEFR | More than 250 free tasks from A1 to C2 [25], organised in Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Business and "Just for Fun" workbooks. Learners can also write custom tasks [23]. Results are reported on the CEFR scale [26][32]. Test Zone has 60 IELTS Academic and 40 General Training tasks [24]. **There are no lessons.** Cambridge describes it as a pedagogical tool and practice environment, "not a text editing facility", that "can be used with or without a teacher" [26]. **CEFR range claimed: A1–C2.** | Help centre, official |
| 4 | Beginner experience | A Beginner workbook exists [23], but a learner starting from zero cannot use the tool. It needs English text as input and offers no instruction, no alphabet teaching and no L1 explanations. The UI is in English and Spanish only [22][38]. The scoring model is less accurate at A1 [34]. | Official, academic |
| 5 | Advanced (C1–C2) | An Advanced workbook exists [23] and scores go up to C2 [32]. **Caveats:** The 2018 model disagreed more with human raters at A1 and C2, and tended to over-predict B1–C1 [34]. It is harder for C-level users to raise their score [34]. The tool scores statistical features, not meaning, and native-level writing can score low because it is "too good" [29]. A short, simple task cannot earn a C1 score [30]. A secondary source says C1–C2 scores are "less stable" and that the models were updated in 2024. That claim is unverified [38]. | Academic, help centre, independent directory |
| 6 | Writing exercises & feedback | Learners get a summative CEFR estimate, word-level flags (spelling, grammar, vocabulary) and sentence-level shading (white = good, solid = needs work, crossed = better but improvable). They also get 268 encouraging "overview" messages and a progress graph [26][28]. Errors are flagged only when the system is more than 90% confident [26]. It does not catch every error and is "designed not to show too many errors" [27]. The 2018 paper says the system is "context-aware" and checks whether a response is relevant to the prompt [34], and the 2016 launch coverage said feedback covered "spelling, vocabulary, grammar and style" [36]. **Feedback on argument, organisation or coherence is not documented** in the help articles consulted [26][27]. Participants (n = 31) in a small 2026 qualitative study described the feedback as grammar-centred, less contextual and "too 'bot or AI'" [35]. A directory calls it fairly generic, focused on CEFR level and grammar rather than style [38]. Test Zone gives an IELTS band score [24]. The help centre has an article titled "How do I add my own feedback to my students' writing?", which suggests teachers can add their own feedback; the article body and its tie to Class View were not verified [33]. | Help centre, academic, independent directory, official |
| 7 | Speaking & pronunciation | **None** in Write & Improve [22]. | Official |
| 8 | AI integration | Supervised machine learning trained on the 30-million-word, error-annotated Cambridge Learner Corpus [26], developed with iLexIR [36]. The 2018 scoring model reported Pearson r = 0.765, Spearman ρ = 0.773 and κ = 0.738 against human ratings, trained and tuned on 2,052 texts and tested on 260 [34]. A directory says it is not a style coach and points learners to generative assistants for stylistic depth [38]. The core automated feedback is free [22][25]; Test Zone band scoring is paid [24]. | Help centre, academic, press, directory |
| 9 | Personalization / adaptivity | Learners choose their own workbook. The 2016 launch coverage said feedback adapts to level [36], and the help centre calls overview feedback "personal, adaptive and formative" (vendor claim) [26]. There is a progress graph, but no adaptive curriculum [26]. | Help centre, press |
| 10 | UI & usability | A web app that works in phone and tablet browsers. There is **no native app**, only a home-screen shortcut [31]. Interface in English and Spanish [22]. Resubmitting suits process writing [37]. Usage data from 517,285 texts shows a mean at the low end of B1, and that users favour beginner prompts [34]. | Help centre, official, academic |
| 11 | Subscription & pricing | The core tool is free and can be used without registering [22][25]. **+Test Zone:** £4.50 a month per workbook (GBP; seen 2026-10-30 on the IELTS page, which does not mention VAT) [24]; the 2022 help article says prices include VAT [25]. **+Class View:** £11 a month for the first 10 members, then £1.10 (next 40), £0.90 (next 50) or £0.80 per additional member, with online purchases capped at 400 members and Test Zone included at no extra cost. This comes from a help article dated 20 Jan 2022 [25]. **Conflict:** a directory checked in July 2026 says Cambridge no longer publishes list prices and offers a 2-month free Class View trial for up to 400 learners [38]. The official IELTS page still shows £4.50 [24]. | Official, help centre, directory |
| 12 | Strengths & weaknesses | **Strengths:** free; a Cambridge research pedigree; CEFR benchmarking; a conservative, high-precision approach that encourages self-correction [26][34]. **Weaknesses:** no instruction; feedback only at word and sentence level; weaker at A1 and C2; English and Spanish UI only; dated help content (2017–2022) [26][27][34][35]. | Mixed |
| 13 | Retention mechanisms | Encouraging graded feedback compared with the previous attempt, plus a progress graph [26]. **No streaks or notifications were found** (not verified). The tool relies on intrinsic motivation and teacher assignment. | Help centre |
| 14 | Accessibility | The footer links to an accessibility statement on the cambridgeassessment.org.uk site [22]. The statement could not be opened in this session (earlier attempts returned 403; a re-check of cambridgeassessment.org.uk/accessibility/ returned 404), so its content is **not verified**. With no native app, there are no store accessibility declarations. | Official |
| 15 | Offline | **None.** It is a cloud-based web app [31][36], and offline use is not documented. | Help centre, press |
| 16 | English course taught from Hebrew | **None.** The UI is in English and Spanish only [22][38], and feedback is in English. | Official, directory |

**What they do better than anyone.** Credible, free, CEFR-referenced automated scoring backed by peer-reviewed validation [34]. Its feedback design (precision over recall, flag without correcting, release feedback gradually) is the most pedagogically deliberate in this group [26][27].

**Where they fall short for a Hebrew-speaking adult aiming at C1–C2 writing.** It assesses rather than teaches. It does not explain *why* something is wrong [26][27]. Apart from a check that the response is relevant to the prompt [34], no feedback on thesis, argument structure, counter-argument, cohesion or register is documented [26][27][35][38]. It is least reliable exactly at the C2 end [34], and C-level users struggle to see their scores move [34]. There is no Hebrew support [22]. A Hebrew speaker's typical errors, such as articles, prepositions and tense or aspect, would be flagged at best and never explained against Hebrew contrasts.

| # | Dimension | Findings | Evidence type |

## Cross-competitor observations

1. **No one teaches English *from Hebrew*.** None of the four offers a Hebrew UI or Hebrew explanations [1][7][12][22][39][48]. LingQ supports Hebrew only as a target language [1]. Quizlet allows user-made Hebrew–English sets of unknown quality [14]. For Lucid's audience, this is a clear and verifiable gap in this group.
2. **No one covers zero → C2 *with productive feedback*.** LingQ starts at roughly A2/B1 and is receptive [8][9]. Quizlet has no curriculum [13]. Write & Improve gives feedback but no instruction [26]. British Council teaches to C1 but gives no feedback on the free route [42][43]. The stages are spread across products, and none of them bridges input to output.
3. **C1–C2 argumentative writing is effectively unserved.** The best automated feedback here (Write & Improve) works at word and sentence level, gives no correct answers and no documented argument feedback, and its validation is weakest at C2 [26][27][34]. British Council supplies C1 model essays with no feedback loop [43]. LingQ relies on native-speaker or tutor corrections paid with points [2][6][8]. Quizlet has nothing [13].
4. **Feedback design lessons from Cambridge.** Flagging only above 90% confidence, releasing errors gradually, and pushing self-correction are deliberate choices [26][27]. Learners still find the feedback generic and "bot-like" when it lacks context and explanation [35]. Lucid should combine high-precision flags with *explanations*, including contrasts with Hebrew.
5. **AI sits in the top tiers.** LingQ's advanced AI is in Premium Plus ($29.99 a month) [2]. British Council's full AI speaking access is in Platinum [51]. Third parties list Quizlet's AI as Plus [15]. Quizlet's conversational tutor was withdrawn entirely [17]. AI writing coaching at C1–C2 is not offered by any of the four.
6. **Paywall backlash is real.** Paywalls, ads, upgrade prompts and billing complaints dominate Quizlet's Trustpilot reviews (1.4/5 from 662 reviews) [16][20], despite 4.6–4.8 app-store ratings [11][12]. Trustpilot reviewers are self-selected, so this shows the shape of complaints, not their share among all users. LingQ users report trouble cancelling and content deleted on cancellation [6][8].
7. **Accessibility is undeclared across the board.** None of the six App Store listings checked for this declares any accessibility features [7][11][12][48][50][55]. None of the accessibility statements that could be reached gave verifiable conformance details.
8. **Offline is rare.** Only LingQ advertises offline study [6][7]. Write & Improve is cloud-only [31]. British Council offline support was not verified beyond PDF worksheets [45].
9. **Retention ranges from absent to pushy.** British Council and Write & Improve have almost no habit mechanics [26][40]. One LingQ App Store reviewer found the reminders too strong [6]. Quizlet's prompts are mostly about upgrading [20]. There is room for humane, progress-based retention, for example a visible CEFR-descriptor journey.
10. **Credentialing and trust.** Cambridge (CEFR-validated scoring [34]) and British Council (EnglishScore certificate [56]) have brand authority that a new entrant lacks. Lucid needs transparent validation of any level estimates it shows.
