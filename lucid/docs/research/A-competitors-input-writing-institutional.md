# Team A — Competitor profiles: Input, flashcards, automated writing feedback & institutional (LingQ, Quizlet, Cambridge Write & Improve, British Council LearnEnglish ecosystem)

> **Method.** Desk research carried out on 2026-10-30. Every claim below cites a page that was opened with WebFetch during this session; search-result snippets alone were not used as evidence. Sources are vendor sites and help centres, Apple App Store listings (US and Israel storefronts), independent and competitor-authored reviews, press coverage, Trustpilot, and peer-reviewed or conference papers. Vendor marketing claims are marked **(vendor claim)**. Reviews written by competing products (StoryLearning, Lingopie, Nibble, My Engineering Buddy) are flagged, because they have a commercial reason to be negative. Prices are as seen on 2026-10-30, with currency and storefront.
>
> **Limitations.** No accounts were created and no product was tested hands-on, so every finding comes from documentation, listings and third-party reports. quizlet.com and help.quizlet.com returned HTTP 403, so Quizlet's official pricing and feature-tier pages could **not** be verified. The Quizlet price figures come from app stores and third parties. The British Council Israel site, the British Council accessibility statement, and the Cambridge accessibility statement returned 503, 404 or 403 errors. The LingQ help centre returned 404, and the AppleVis forum thread on LingQ accessibility returned 403. Google Play listings could not be read. Many Write & Improve help articles date from 2017 to 2022 and may not reflect later model updates. App Store "what's new" entries show no year. WebFetch returns model-summarised page text, so wording is paraphrased and small extraction errors are possible. Reddit was not consulted. About 29 fetch attempts failed and are not cited.

## LingQ

| # | Dimension | Findings | Evidence type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Target audience | Built for self-directed learners of 50+ languages (53 per the site) who want to learn from authentic content (vendor claim) [1]. Independent and competitor reviews agree that it fits learners at roughly lower-intermediate level and above who already have material they want to read or hear. They say it does not suit beginners who want guidance or learners whose focus is speaking [8][9][10]. The iOS app is rated 13+ [7]. | Official, independent review, competitor review, app store |
| 2 | Learning methodology | Comprehensible input through reading and listening. Learners tap unknown words to save them as "LingQs" with their context. Saved words move through four stages, from New to Known. Review uses spaced repetition with multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank and dictation items, plus a daily email of due words [8]. LingQ says vocabulary is learned "in context rather than drills or games" (vendor claim) [1]. L1 support is a tap-to-translate dictionary whose language can be changed: an App Store reviewer set it to Spanish but still saw English definitions [6]. The quality of Hebrew glosses was not verified. The UI is not available in Hebrew [1][7]. | Competitor review, vendor blog, app store |

Learners can import any article, YouTube video, podcast or ebook, and AI transcription turns audio into lessons [1][6][8]. Some content is shared by members, so its quality varies [8]. | Official, app store, competitor review |

Ratings: 4.7 from 10K ratings on the US App Store [6]; 4.7 from 193 ratings on the Israel App Store [7]; 4.5 on Google Play from 50.6K reviews, as reported by StoryLearning [8]. | Competitor reviews, vendor blog, app store |
| 11 | Subscription & pricing | **lingq.com (USD, seen 2026-10-30):** The free plan allows 20 saved LingQs, 5 imported lessons and 1 playlist. Premium Plus costs $29.99 a month or $269.99 a year [2]. **Israel App Store (ILS, seen 2026-10-30):** Premium ₪54.00 a month, ₪290.00 for 6 months or ₪429.90 for 12 months; Premium Plus ₪107.90 a month or ₪969.90 a year [7]. **US App Store:** a 6-month plan at $80.99, plus unlabelled SKUs at $109.99 and $99.99 [6]. StoryLearning (Sept 2026) reports UK prices of £12.99 a month and £103.99 a year, a 30-day refund for new Premium members, and that content is deleted when Premium is cancelled [8]. One review mentions a "lifetime" plan at about $200 [10]. That plan is **not** on the official pricing page [2], so it is unverified. | Official, app store, reviews |

| 13 | Retention mechanisms | Coins, a daily goal and streak rewards. Statistics and challenges are Premium features [8]. There are also streaks, rankings, word, book and 90-day challenges, and community forums [9]. **What they do better than anyone.** LingQ has the best pipeline in this group for turning *any* authentic text, audio or video into a study unit, with tap-to-save vocabulary, transcripts and spaced repetition. For B2→C2 *receptive* growth (reading and listening volume), nothing else here comes close [1][6][8].

**Where they fall short for a Hebrew-speaking adult aiming at C1–C2 writing.** There is no Hebrew interface and no Hebrew-medium instruction [1][7]. It cannot take a learner from zero, including the Latin alphabet [5][9]. There is no grammar sequence and no instruction in argumentation. Writing feedback depends on peer corrections paid with points, not on criteria-based feedback [2][6]. The learner must design their own path, and reviewers repeatedly name that as the main point of failure [5][8][9].

Its flagship conversational AI (Q-Chat) was withdrawn [17]. Monetisation through daily caps and ads hurts trust [16][20]. For Lucid, it is a model for how spaced-repetition vocabulary review *should feel*, and a warning about paywall backlash.

## Cambridge Write & Improve (writeandimprove.com)

| # | Dimension | Findings | Evidence type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Target audience | EFL learners who want writing practice without a teacher [36]. Teachers and organisations use +Class View, and IELTS and B2 First candidates use +Test Zone [22][25]. TESL Ontario lists ESL/EAL adults, secondary students and EAP learners [37]. | Official, help centre, press, professional association |
| 2 | Learning methodology | Write, get an instant automated assessment, revise and resubmit (a process-writing loop) [26][37]. Feedback is "indirect, semi-corrective": problems are flagged but **no correct answers are given**, so learners have to self-correct [26]. Feedback is released gradually so learners are not overwhelmed [26][27]. | Help centre, professional association |
| 3 | Curriculum / CEFR | More than 250 free tasks from A1 to C2 [25], organised in Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Business and "Just for Fun" workbooks. Learners can also write custom tasks [23]. Results are reported on the CEFR scale [26][32]. Test Zone has 60 IELTS Academic and 40 General Training tasks [24]. **There are no lessons.** Cambridge describes it as a practice tool, "not a text editor", and not a replacement for a teacher [26]. **CEFR range claimed: A1–C2.** | Help centre, official |
| 4 | Beginner experience | A Beginner workbook exists [23], but a learner starting from zero cannot use the tool. It needs English text as input and offers no instruction, no alphabet teaching and no L1 explanations. The UI is in English and Spanish only [22][38]. The scoring model is less accurate at A1 [34]. | Official, academic |
| 5 | Advanced (C1–C2) | An Advanced workbook exists and scores go up to C2 [26]. **Caveats:** The 2018 model disagreed more with human raters at A1 and C2, and tended to over-predict B1–C1 [34]. It is harder for C-level users to raise their score [34]. The tool scores statistical features, not meaning, and native-level writing can score low because it is "too good" [29]. A short, simple task cannot earn a C1 score [30]. A secondary source says C1–C2 scores are "less stable" and that the models were updated in 2024. That claim is unverified [38]. | Academic, help centre, independent directory |
| 6 | Writing exercises & feedback | Learners get a summative CEFR estimate, word-level flags (spelling, grammar, vocabulary) and sentence-level shading (white = good, solid = needs work, crossed = better but improvable). They also get 268 encouraging "overview" messages and a progress graph [26][28]. Errors are flagged only when the system is more than 90% confident [26]. It does not catch every error and deliberately limits how many it shows [27]. **No feedback on content, argument or coherence is documented** [27]. Learners in a 2026 study described the feedback as grammar-centred, not very contextual and "too bot or AI" [35]. A directory calls it generic, focused on level and grammar rather than style [38]. Test Zone gives an estimated IELTS band [24]. Teachers using Class View can add their own feedback [33]. | Help centre, academic, independent directory, official |
| 7 | Speaking & pronunciation | **None** in Write & Improve [22]. | Official |
| 8 | AI integration | Supervised machine learning trained on the 30-million-word, error-annotated Cambridge Learner Corpus [26], developed with iLexIR [36]. The 2018 scoring model reported Pearson r = 0.765, Spearman ρ = 0.773 and κ = 0.738 against human ratings, trained on about 2,052 texts [34]. It is **not a generative coach** [38]. All AI features are free. | Help centre, academic, press, directory |
| 9 | Personalization / adaptivity | Learners choose their own workbook. The 2016 launch coverage said feedback adapts to level (vendor claim) [36]. There is a progress graph, but no adaptive curriculum [26]. | Help centre, press |
| 10 | UI & usability | A web app that works in phone and tablet browsers. There is **no native app**, only a home-screen shortcut [31]. Interface in English and Spanish [22]. Resubmitting suits process writing [37]. Usage data from 517,285 texts shows a mean at the low end of B1, and that users favour beginner prompts [34]. | Help centre, official, academic |
| 11 | Subscription & pricing | The core tool is free and can be used without registering [22][25]. **+Test Zone:** £4.50 a month per workbook (GBP, VAT included; seen 2026-10-30 on the IELTS page) [24][25]. **+Class View:** £11 a month for the first 10 members, then £1.10, £0.90 or £0.80 per member by tier. This comes from a help article dated 20 Jan 2022 [25]. **Conflict:** a directory checked in July 2026 says Cambridge no longer publishes list prices, offers a 2-month free trial for up to 400 learners, and bundles Test Zone into Class View [38]. The official IELTS page still shows £4.50 [24]. | Official, help centre, directory |
| 12 | Strengths & weaknesses | **Strengths:** free; a Cambridge research pedigree; CEFR benchmarking; a conservative, high-precision approach that encourages self-correction [26][34]. **Weaknesses:** no instruction; feedback only at word and sentence level; weaker at A1 and C2; English and Spanish UI only; dated help content (2017–2022) [26][27][34][35]. | Mixed |
| 13 | Retention mechanisms | Encouraging graded feedback compared with the previous attempt, plus a progress graph [26]. **No streaks or notifications were found** (not verified). The tool relies on intrinsic motivation and teacher assignment. | Help centre |
| 14 | Accessibility | The footer links to a Cambridge accessibility statement [22]. That link redirected to cambridge.org/accessibility, which returned HTTP 403, so it is **not verified**. With no native app, there are no store accessibility declarations. | Official |
| 15 | Offline | **None.** It is a cloud-based web app [31][36], and offline use is not documented. | Help centre, press |
| 16 | English course taught from Hebrew | **None.** The UI is in English and Spanish only [22][38], and feedback is in English. | Official, directory |

**What they do better than anyone.** Credible, free, CEFR-referenced automated scoring backed by peer-reviewed validation [34]. Its feedback design (precision over recall, flag without correcting, release feedback gradually) is the most pedagogically deliberate in this group [26][27].

**Where they fall short for a Hebrew-speaking adult aiming at C1–C2 writing.** It assesses rather than teaches. It does not explain *why* something is wrong [26][27]. It does not assess thesis, argument structure, counter-argument, cohesion or register [27][35][38]. It is least reliable exactly at the C2 end [34], and C-level users struggle to see their scores move [34]. There is no Hebrew support [22]. A Hebrew speaker's typical errors, such as articles, prepositions and tense or aspect, would be flagged at best and never explained against Hebrew contrasts.

## Cross-competitor observations

1. **No one teaches English *from Hebrew*.** None of the four offers a Hebrew UI or Hebrew explanations [1][7][12][22][39][48]. LingQ supports Hebrew only as a target language [1]. Quizlet allows user-made Hebrew–English sets of unknown quality [14]. For Lucid's audience, this is a clear and verifiable gap in this group.
2. **No one covers zero → C2 *with productive feedback*.** LingQ starts at roughly A2/B1 and is receptive [8][9]. Quizlet has no curriculum [13]. Write & Improve gives feedback but no instruction [26]. British Council teaches to C1 but gives no feedback on the free route [42][43]. The stages are spread across products, and none of them bridges input to output.
3. **C1–C2 argumentative writing is effectively unserved.** The best automated feedback here (Write & Improve) works at word and sentence level, gives no explanations or argument feedback, and its validation is weakest at C2 [26][27][34]. British Council supplies C1 model essays with no feedback loop [43]. LingQ relies on peer corrections paid with points [2]. Quizlet has nothing [13].
4. **Feedback design lessons from Cambridge.** Flagging only above 90% confidence, releasing errors gradually, and pushing self-correction are deliberate choices [26][27]. Learners still find the feedback generic and "bot-like" when it lacks context and explanation [35]. Lucid should combine high-precision flags with *explanations*, including contrasts with Hebrew.
5. **AI sits in the top tiers.** LingQ's advanced AI is in Premium Plus ($29.99 a month) [2]. British Council's full AI speaking access is in Platinum [51]. Third parties list Quizlet's AI as Plus [15]. Quizlet's conversational tutor was withdrawn entirely [17]. AI writing coaching at C1–C2 is not offered by any of the four.
6. **Paywall backlash is real.** Quizlet's daily caps and ads are tied to a Trustpilot score of 1.4/5 [16][20], despite 4.6–4.8 app-store ratings [11][12]. LingQ users report trouble cancelling and content deleted on cancellation [6][8].
7. **Accessibility is undeclared across the board.** None of the six App Store listings checked for this declares any accessibility features [7][11][12][48][50][55]. None of the accessibility statements that could be reached gave verifiable conformance details.
8. **Offline is rare.** Only LingQ advertises offline study [6][7]. Write & Improve is cloud-only [31]. British Council offline support was not verified beyond PDF worksheets [45].
9. **Retention ranges from absent to pushy.** British Council and Write & Improve have almost no habit mechanics [26][40]. LingQ's reminders are called "pushy" [6]. Quizlet's prompts are mostly about upgrading [20]. There is room for humane, progress-based retention, for example a visible CEFR-descriptor journey.
10. **Credentialing and trust.** Cambridge (CEFR-validated scoring [34]) and British Council (EnglishScore certificate [56]) have brand authority that a new entrant lacks. Lucid needs transparent validation of any level estimates it shows.
