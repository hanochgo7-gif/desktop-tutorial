The pit is the foundation of the station

Every station starts with a hole in the ground, and what is done in it determines whether the tanks sit right, whether the forecourt holds and whether the water drains where it should. A tank pit must be excavated to exact dimensions and depth, with suitable bedding, containment liners and anchoring, and at sites with a high water table, dewatering during the work.

We do the earthworks ourselves, with our own heavy equipment and permanent crews, so excavation, backfill, compaction and site development are coordinated with the rest of the work and do not depend on a subcontractor.

How it works

Earthworks in five stages

  1. 01Survey and planning

    Marking out, level surveys and planning depths and slopes.

  2. 02Excavation

    Controlled excavation for the tank pit and the trenches, including dewatering if needed.

  3. 03Bedding and liner

    Suitable bedding, containment liners and tank anchoring.

  4. 04Backfill and compaction

    Backfill in layers and compaction, tested per the requirements.

  5. 05Development and paving

    Drainage, walls, concrete forecourts and paving through to handover.

FAQ

Questions about Earthworks

Do you also do earthworks that are not part of building a station?

Yes. Earthworks, site development, drainage and paving are a field in their own right for us, for fuel and LPG stations, data centers and infrastructure sites.

What happens when the water table is high?

The tank pit is excavated with dewatering during the work, and the tanks are anchored against flotation. This is set during planning, based on the soil survey.

Who supplies the equipment?

We do. We have our own heavy equipment and permanent crews, so the schedule does not depend on the availability of subcontractors.
