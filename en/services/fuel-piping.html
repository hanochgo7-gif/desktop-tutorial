The piping is where a station leaks

Most leaks at fuel stations do not start in the tank but in the piping and the connections: a worn section, a flange that was not sealed, a joint closed in concrete without being tested. That is why we treat the piping as the station's critical system: pipe within a pipe, connections only inside sealed sumps, and every section pressure and tightness tested before it is closed.

The company started in 1972 as a piping and infrastructure contractor, and that is still the foundation of everything we do. Piping connections we made decades ago are still tight and in service.

What the service includes

All types of fuel piping

  • Fuel and vapor pipingSuction, fill and vent lines from the tanks to the dispensers.
  • Double-wall pipingPipe within a pipe, with monitoring of the interstitial space and leak detection.
  • Fiberglass pipingFiberglass fuel piping, as installed at the Nover station in Ashdod.
  • Stage II vapor recovery pipingVapor piping routes from the dispensers to the tank.
  • Connections in sealed sumpsPiping connections only inside sealed sumps under the dispensers and above the tanks.
  • Fill systems and valvesSpill containment, valves, flanges and double-wall fill systems.
  • Generator feed linesN+1 feed lines for emergency generators in data centers.
  • Testing and documentationPressure and tightness testing of every section, with documentation handed to the client.
How it works

From the route to the test, in five stages

  1. 01Route planning

    Planning the piping route against the plans of the station, the tanks and the dispensers.

  2. 02Trenching

    Trenches at the required depths and slopes, with a sand bed.

  3. 03Laying and connecting

    Laying the piping, connections inside sealed sumps, fill systems and valves.

  4. 04Pressure and tightness testing

    Testing every section before backfill, with full documentation.

  5. 05Backfill and handover

    Controlled backfill, pouring, and handover of the test documentation.

FAQ

Questions about Fuel Piping

What is the difference between single-wall and double-wall piping?

In double-wall piping the fuel line runs inside a containment pipe, and the space between them is monitored. A leak from the inner pipe is stopped by the containment and detected, instead of reaching the ground.

Can only the piping be replaced, without replacing the tanks?

Yes, when the tanks are sound. We open the piping route, replace it with double-wall piping and sealed sumps, and test for tightness before closing.

What do you receive when the work is done?

Documentation of the pressure and tightness tests for every piping section, required for licensing and used by the station over the years.
