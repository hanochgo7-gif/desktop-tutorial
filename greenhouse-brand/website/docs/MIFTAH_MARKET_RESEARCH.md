## 3. What international buyers look for

**B1. A local track record and local management**
- Savills' farmland-investor checklist asks whether the area "has a track record for the type of agriculture" planned. It also calls access to "local agri-business management who are embedded in the agricultural community" vital.
- Source: https://en.savills.pt/blog/article/351166/rural-property/how-to-pick-high-performing-farmland-investments---an-investor-s-checklist.aspx (8 Sep 2023) [F].
- **[I]:** a foreign contractor has to show it has worked in comparable conditions *and* how the site is run locally.

**B2. Grant and credit windows set the timetable (Portugal)**
- In April 2021 Portugal opened a €17.5 M support line for anti-hail nets on apple, pear and stone-fruit orchards. Co-financing ran "até 65%" (up to 65%). Applicants had 60 days to apply and two years to build. The grant could be combined with an EIF-guaranteed credit line.
- Source: https://www.portugal.gov.pt/gc22/comunicacao/noticias/governo-anuncia-apoio-de-175-milhoes-de-euros-para-instalacao-de-redes-antigranizo (17 Apr 2021) [F].
- No 2025–2026 notice for hail nets was found [S, search of PEPAC announcements, e.g. https://observador.pt/2025/07/25/abertas-candidaturas-a-426-milhoes-de-euros-no-ambito-do-pepac-no-continente/].
- **[I]:** Portuguese buyers may need a contractor who can produce a quote and technical description in time for a grant window. Whether MIFTAH does this is unknown and must be confirmed before the site says so.

**B3. Structural design for site loads**
- A failure study of hail-protection canopies (over a car park, not an orchard) found they collapsed because of "erroneous design assumptions on snow load and the poor design and construction" of members and connections.
- Source: Vatansever, Seçkin & Yazıcı, *Engineering Failure Analysis* (2017), https://openaccess.iku.edu.tr/handle/11413/2236 (DOI 10.1016/j.engfailanal.2017.04.032) [F].
- A Good Fruit Grower article reports that with stronger netting, the weight of ice "pulled down the supporting poles" [S, https://goodfruit.com/protecting-growers-from-hail; the page came back empty when fetched].
- **[I]:** buyers have reason to ask how a structure is designed for wind, hail or snow at their own site.

**B4. CE marking of structural steel in the EU**
- Since 1 July 2014, CE marking to EN 1090-1 has been mandatory for fabricated structural steelwork with a structural function.
- The indicative scope list includes "industrial and agricultural sheds". EXC1 is given the example of farm buildings.
- Source: https://www.engineersireland.ie/Engineers-Journal/Civil/ce-marking-of-structural-steel-an-update-for-engineers (31 Jan 2017) [F].
- **[I]:** EU buyers of MIFTAH's **loading sheds** may ask about this. Whether pole-and-cable net houses fall within scope was **not verified**. This is an open question for MIFTAH and a notified body. The site should not claim compliance (ledger 3.8: no certifications stated).

**B5. Buyers do most of their research before they make contact**
- Gartner research, as reported, found B2B buyers spend "just 17 percent of their time meeting with suppliers". Of their time, 27% goes to independent online research and 18% to independent offline research.
- Source: https://istart.com.au/news-items/right-info-right-channel-happy-b2b-customer/ (17 Aug 2018) [F for the article. It is a secondary report of Gartner; the Gartner page returned 403].

**B6. Technical advisers are trusted more than sales reps**
- The McKinsey *Global Farmer Insights 2026* survey, as reported: 56% of farmers name technical agronomists as a trusted decision source, against 6% for generative AI or AI search. Sales reps as purchase influencers fell from 67% to 56%.
- Source: https://www.rfdtv.com/farmers-adopt-ai-while-still-trusting-agronomists-most (22 Sep 2026) [F for the article. The McKinsey PDF itself returned 503].

**B7. Formal procurement checks experience on similar contracts**
- When development-finance rules apply (relevant for Africa), works contractors are typically qualified on financial capability, annual construction turnover, general experience and **specific experience of similar contracts**.
- [S] This comes from a secondary summary of World Bank procurement. The current standard procurement document was not opened.

## 4. Synthesis

### 4.1 Patterns

| Pattern | Common (expected) | Differentiating (few do it) | To avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Navigation** | By product or application (Agromalla, Ginegar, ULMA) | **By crop** (Valente, Arrigoni, VOEN). By **structure type** (Paskal "Net Houses"). | Product names nobody searches for |
| **Scope** | Either "we make nets" (Ginegar, Arrigoni, Paskal) or "we install" (Agromalla, Novafrut) | **Design + manufacture + install + after-sales** from one company (Valente, P. Marom). Maintenance and open/close **services** as named items (Agromalla). | Implying a turnkey scope that is not delivered |
| **Project proof** | Photo cards with a title and sometimes a country (Arrigoni, P. Marom) | **A fixed card with crop, country, area and a measured result** (Haygrove, Netafim, earlier pass). An area figure on every case. | Galleries with no filters, no size and no year (P. Marom). Placeholder images (Valente). |
| **Scale claims** | Years and number of countries (Ginegar, Paskal, Valente) | Third-party certificates tied to the product (Valente DNV, Ginegar ISO, Arrigoni SGS) | **JavaScript counters that render 0** (Azrom, Agrotop). Inconsistent years (P. Marom: 30 vs "over two decades"). |
| **CTA** | "Contact us", "Learn more" | Quote-specific CTAs ("Get Quote", Ginegar's quote cart). A downloadable profile (Arrigoni, P. Marom PDFs). Find-your-agent (Haygrove, VOEN). | No contact CTA at all (Arrigoni home). No form (ULMA, Azrom). |
| **Form** | Name, email, phone, message (Agromalla, Ginegar) | **Activity/role + crop + country** as required fields (Valente, Paskal, Arrigoni's role list). "Already a customer?" (Valente). | Forms hosted off-site (P. Marom) |
| **Languages** | Home language plus English | 4–5 languages (Arrigoni, ULMA) | Mixed languages on one page (Valente). Event pop-ups left up for years (Arrigoni, 2020). |

### 4.2 Implications for MIFTAH's site

1. **Organise the Net houses page by crop.** Give each crop a block: banana, kiwi, apple, cherry, avocado (the crops in the brief and ledger 3.1). Under each, show what the structure protects against: hail, sun, insects, wind. Valente, Arrigoni and VOEN all let the grower start from the crop, while installers like Agromalla start from the product (§1.1, §1.5, §1.8).

2. **Say plainly that MIFTAH makes what it builds.** For example: "Designed, made in our own plant, installed by our crews, maintained after handover." Net makers stop at the product (Ginegar, Arrigoni, Paskal) and installers stop at installation (Agromalla, Novafrut). Only Valente and P. Marom claim the whole chain (§1). This rests on ledger 1.2 and 1.7.

3. **Use one fixed project card:** crop, country, area (m² or ha), scope (design / manufacture / install / local team) and year where known. Fill it only with ledger figures, such as Mexico 150,000 m² (ledger 3.2). The pattern comes from Haygrove and Netafim (earlier pass). The gaps to avoid are P. Marom's unfiltered gallery and Arrigoni's single numbers (§1.2, §1.5). Add a filter by country and crop once there are more than about 6 cards.

4. **Write numbers as plain HTML text, not animated counters.** "410,000 m² abroad in 5 countries" should be readable without JavaScript. Azrom and Agrotop counters show 0 or negative values to crawlers and some visitors (§1.8; earlier pass §1.7). Use one experience figure everywhere ("20+ years", ledger 1.6). P. Marom gives two different figures (§1.2).

5. **Add a short "How we design for your site" block.** Cover the site visit, wind and hail exposure, pole and cable sizing, and anchoring. Do not name a standard or certificate MIFTAH has not confirmed. Buyers have evidence that canopies fail when load assumptions are wrong (B3), and competitors use spec detail as proof (Valente posts and mesh, §1.1). Ask MIFTAH whether its loading-shed steel needs EN 1090 CE marking for EU jobs (B4). This is an open question, not site copy.

6. **List maintenance as named services.** Possible items: annual inspection, net replacement, seasonal opening and closing. Only list what MIFTAH confirms. Maintenance itself is in the ledger (§2: "Maintenance of structures and systems: Us"). Agromalla is the only competitor that sells upkeep as a menu of services, and Novafrut's pitch is lower annual upkeep (§1.3, §1.4).

7. **Make the form do the screening.**
   - Fields: name, company, **I am** (private investor / agricultural company / entrepreneur / grower), **country**, **crop**, **area** (ha or dunam, with the 30 ha abroad and 5 ha Israel minimum stated beside it), **what you need** (net house / windbreak / loading shed / turnkey farm / maintenance), **project stage** (land secured? already planted?), phone/email and message.
   - Valente and Paskal require role or activity, crop and country. Arrigoni segments by role (§1.1, §1.5, §1.7).
   - The ledger confirms minimum sizes and price-only enquiries as the main time-wasters (ledger 4.2, 4.3).
   - Keep the form on the main domain, not on a third-party subdomain like P. Marom's (§1.2).

8. **Use specific CTA wording.** The main CTA should be something like "Send us your plot details" or "Request a site assessment", not just "Contact". Put click-to-call next to it, because phone is MIFTAH's real channel today (ledger 4.8). The weakest sites in §1 offer only "Saber más" or no CTA at all (§1.3, §1.5). Buyers arrive already well researched (B5), so the next step has to be concrete.

9. **Answer "who runs it locally?" on every project abroad.** Lines such as "local manufacture, shipping and local crews" (Belarus, ledger 3.3) and "local team management" (ledger 3.2) answer the investor's question about embedded local management (Savills, B1). Van der Hoeven makes the same point as "local workers under supervision" (earlier pass §1.3).

10. **Put the technical people forward, not sales copy.** Name who designs the structure and who handles agronomy on farm projects, once approved (ledger 1.14, 4.14). Farmers trust technical agronomists far more than sales reps (B6). Competitors with named growers in testimonials (Valente) read as more credible (§1.1). Only use quotes after approval (ledger 3.7).

11. **Make a downloadable one-page profile for Fruit Logistica.** It should cover net houses, the project map figures and the minimum sizes. Link it from the Contact page and print a QR code on the stand material. Arrigoni ("Download COMPANY PROFILE SLIM"), P. Marom's project PDFs and Rivulis's prospectus (earlier pass) use this pattern, and trade-fair contacts follow up later by email. Take the fair banner down after 5 Feb 2027 so it does not go stale like Arrigoni's 2020 pop-ups (§1.5).

12. **Keep every page in one language.** Publish English and Hebrew as separate versions (ledger 4.9) and never mix them on a page, which is Valente's flaw (§1.1). **[I]** For Portugal and Spain, a translated PDF profile may be a cheaper first step than a third language on the site, since the Spanish and Italian competitors publish in their buyers' language (§1.1, §1.3, §1.5).

**Open questions for MIFTAH raised by this research:**
- Can it produce quotes and technical documents in the form grant schemes need, such as Portugal's 65% co-financing (B2)?
- Are there any engineering calculations or material certificates it can show (B3, B4)?
- Which maintenance services does it sell as separate items (implication 6)?
