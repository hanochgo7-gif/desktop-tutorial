Net houses and farm structures

We design, manufacture and install net houses for orchards and bananas, and the loading sheds and windbreaks around them. One team from the first site visit to maintenance.

By crop and by use

Banana net houses

Bananas

Net
11% shade net: light and very airy.
Protects against
Wind and hail in winter. In summer it saves water by reducing evaporation, and gives the plants better growing conditions.
Structure
Large spans between poles, so a single structure can cover up to 10 ha (100 dunam). Trellising and plant support are part of the structure.
Why it pays
The light net and wide spans use less material than other net houses.

Orchard net houses

Apple, cherry, kiwi, avocado, persimmon

Net
10% to 30% shade, chosen for the crop.
Protects against
Hail and sunburn during flowering and fruit set, when trees are most sensitive and a single storm can cut the yield.
Structure
Built so the nets fold away in winter and spread in summer. Automatic folding systems can be added.
Trellising
Support systems for the crop, such as a pergola-shaped trellis for kiwi.

Loading sheds and windbreaks

For the farm around the orchard

Loading sheds
Shade sheds for loading, unloading and handling produce. Wide spans leave room for trucks and carts to turn.
Net to order
Shade percentage and colour chosen with you. An opening and closing system can be added for bad weather.
Windbreaks
Designed and built with the rest of the farm.

Maintenance, dismantling and reuse

For structures already in the field

Maintenance
Care for structures as nets, cables and fittings weather over the years.
Dismantling
Taking structures down, and processing and preparing the material for a new structure when needed.
One stage only
Needs mapping, design, manufacture, supervision, training or maintenance can each be ordered on their own.

The parts of the structure

  1. 1
    Net10–30% shade for orchards, against hail and sunburn at flowering and fruit set. 11% for bananas, light and airy.
  2. 2
    Steel cablesTensioned across the pole heads to carry the net.
  3. 3
    Galvanized steel polesMade in our plant, set in rows at the span and height the design calls for.
  4. 4
    Perimeter anchoringEdge cables tie the structure to the ground against wind.
  5. 5
    Trellising and supportBuilt with the structure for the crop: a pergola trellis for kiwi, support for bananas.
  6. 6
    FoldingOn orchards, nets can fold away in winter and spread in summer, by hand or automatically.

Designed for the site, not from a catalogue

Before a structure is drawn, we look at the crop, the soil, drainage, access for trucks and the local wind regime. Loads are calculated per project, and the bill of quantities balances the amount of steel and net against strength and budget.

From site visit to maintenance

The same four steps on every project. Each one can also be ordered on its own.

  1. 01

    Needs mapping on site

    We meet you on the land and map the crop, soil, location, drainage, access roads and wind before anything is drawn.

  2. 02

    Engineering design

    Our practical engineers in construction calculate the loads and produce the drawings and a detailed bill of quantities, so material, strength and budget are balanced.

  3. 03

    Manufacturing in our plant

    After the quote is approved, poles, nets, cables and fittings are made, packed for the site and checked before they leave.

  4. 04

    Installation and maintenance

    Our crews, certified for work at height, build the structure with the trellising the crop needs, and maintain it as the materials weather.

Abroad. On projects outside Israel we plan the work, train and manage local crews, and run execution on site. Components usually come from our plant in Israel; in Belarus, manufacturing was organised locally.
