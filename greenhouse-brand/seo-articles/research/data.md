## 1. Hail nets on orchards (apple, pear, cherry)

### 1.1 Apple yield under a hail net vs open sky, including a hail year [1 trial]
- **Figures ('Royal Gala', Vacaria, RS, Brazil, black hail net, 4×7 mm mesh), yield t/ha:**
  - 2008/09: open 59.3 · net 90.0 (significant)
  - 2009/10: open 30.9 · net 36.2 (not significant)
  - 2010/11: open = **no harvest (total loss from one hailstorm)** · net 95.2
- **Other microclimate effects:** PAR −32.8%; wind speed −30%; no change in air temperature, humidity or rainfall.
- **Source:** Bosco, L.C., Bergamaschi, H., Cardoso, L.S., de Paula, V.A., Marodin, G.A.B., Brauner, P.C. "Microclimate alterations caused by agricultural hail net coverage and effects on apple tree yield in subtropical climate of Southern Brazil." *Bragantia* 77(1), Instituto Agronômico de Campinas, 2018. https://www.redalyc.org/journal/908/90859320018/
- **Quotes:** "The hail nets reduced PAR by 32.8% and the wind speed by 30%. In contrast, coverage did not alter the air temperature, humidity or rainfall." · "The yield of apples tends to be higher under hail net and this is more pronounced when one hailstorm event occur." · "there was no harvest for the 2010/2011 cycle due to a total loss of fruits caused by one hailstorm event."
- **Caveat:** The table values were read through a page-reading tool (the PDF was blocked to direct download). The paper says fruit under the net also had some damage, "mostly mild", but it still went to the fresh market. Do not claim "zero damage".

### 1.2 Pear orchard after a hailstorm, net vs no net [1 trial]
- **Figures (north-eastern North Macedonia, 2014–2015, transparent anti-hail net, 'Bartlett' and 'Red Bartlett'):** on **uncovered** trees, fallen fruit was 40% ('Bartlett') and 48% ('Red Bartlett'). Only 5–8% of fruit was undamaged. Covered trees got "maximum protection".
- **Source:** Kiprijanovski, M., Gjamovski, V., Arsov, T. "The effects of anti-hail net in protection of pear orchard after hailstorm occurrence." *Acta Horticulturae* 1139, ISHS, 2016. https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2016.1139.91 (abstract: https://www.ishs.org/ishs-article/1139_91)
- **Quote:** "Percentage of fallen fruits on uncovered trees ranged from 40% in 'Bartlett' to 48% in 'Red Bartlett'. Only 5 to 8% of the fruits were undamaged. … On the covered trees maximum protection from hail was obtained." · "an anti-hail protective net demonstrated its efficiency against hail, increased fruit quality and productivity in the same and in the next production year."
- **Do not use:** a vendor/academia snippet giving 14.65 vs 0.60 kg/tree. It could not be verified (page blocked).

### 1.3 Sunburn under hail and shade nets (apple)
- **Australia (hail netting, multi-site trials, QLD/NSW/VIC):**
  - Granny Smith, Drouin (VIC), April 1997: **21%** of fruit sunburnt on uncovered trees (9% severe) vs **6%** under net (1% severe).
  - Fuji, Granite Belt (QLD), 1998: **36%** sunburnt uncovered vs **8%** under white netting.
  - Hail net microclimate: PAR −12% to −27%; up to 50% lower wind speed; daytime temperature 1–3 °C lower on hot days; no frost protection.
  - **Source:** Middleton, S.G., McWaters, A.D. "Maximising Apple Orchard Productivity under Hail Netting." Final Report, Project AP96014. Queensland Horticulture Institute, Queensland DPI / HRDC, Sept 2000. https://era.dpi.qld.gov.au/id/eprint/8111/1/Maximising%20Apple%20Orchard%20Productivy%20under%20Hail%20Netting%20Project%20AP96014%20Final%20Report%20Sept%202000_.pdf
  - **Quotes:** "in April 1997, 21 % of Granny Smith fruit numbers on uncovered trees at the Drouin site were sunburnt (9% severely), compared to 6% (1 % severely) of fruit under net" · "(36% of Fuji fruit numbers affected), with white netting reducing the sunburn incidence to 8% of the Fuji fruit" · "Reductions in light levels (PAR) of 12-27% … Up to 50% lower windspeed compared to outside netting … Slight reduction in daytime temperatures by 1-3°C on warm to hot days … does not offer frost protection."
- **Washington State, USA [1 trial]:** 'Granny Smith' under 20% white shade net, 2014. **92%** of fruit had no sunburn under the net vs **51%** in the control. By complement, 8% vs 49% of fruit had some sunburn. Max PAR −32%. Soil moisture at 0–30 cm was 20% higher.
  - **Source:** Kalcsits, L., Asteggiano, L., Schmidt, T., Musacchi, S., Serra, S., Layne, D.R., Mupambi, G. "Shade netting reduces sunburn damage and soil moisture depletion in 'Granny Smith' apples." *Acta Horticulturae* 1228, ISHS, 2018. https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2018.1228.11 · PDF: https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/cahnrs/uploads/sites/30/Kalcsits-etal-2018-netting-sunburn-granny.pdf
  - **Quote:** "Netting significantly decreased fruit sunburn, with 92% of harvested fruit having no sunburn damage compared to control in which 51% had no sunburn damage in 2014. Although preliminary…" (the authors themselves call it preliminary)
  - Same paper, background: "In Australia … up to 10% of total fruit can be lost due to sunburn … Under South African growing conditions, producers estimate losses due to sunburn damage at 10-20%, but in some years as high as 30-50% of the total yield."

### 1.4 Review-level facts (apple netting)
- **Mupambi, G. et al. "The influence of protective netting on tree physiology and fruit quality of apple: A review."** *Scientia Horticulturae* 236:60–72, Elsevier, 2018. https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/cahnrs/uploads/sites/30/Mupambi-etal-2018-protective-netting-apple-review.pdf
  - Wind: "Wind speed was reduced by approximately 47% in an apple orchard in Washington State under 20% pearl, red and blue photoselective shade nets (Kalcsits et al., 2017). This corresponds well with Middleton and McWaters (2000) who reported up to 50% lower wind speeds"
  - Water: "McCaskill et al. (2016) estimated potential evapotranspiration to be reduced by 30% under protective netting."
  - Dust and ageing (supports the maintenance message): "Blanke (2009) reported a 2% reduction in PAR transmission through the net per year."
  - Honesty note: "The reported results on the effect of protective netting on fruit quality have not been conclusive."
- **Furuya, D.E.G. et al. "Hail Netting in Apple Orchards: Current Knowledge, Research Gaps, and Perspectives for Digital Agriculture."** *Climate* 13(10):203, MDPI, 2025 (Embrapa). https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/climate/climate-13-00203/article_deploy/climate-13-00203.pdf
  - "Black hail nets are widely adopted in apple orchards due to their proven efficiency in protecting trees from hail damage … black nets include a 63% reduction in PAR" · "This decreased light penetration delays apple maturation by up to one week compared with white nets" · "Economically, black nets have higher annual costs compared with insurance-based hail protection, and, in some cases, reduced fruit coloration negatively impacted profitability". This supports a "net colour and shading % must be engineered per crop" message.
- **Economics (net vs insurance):** Porsch, A., Gandorfer, M., Bitsch, V. "Strategies to manage hail risk in apple production." *Agricultural Finance Review* 78(5):532–550, Emerald, 2018. https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/afrpps/afr-07-2017-0062.html. Finding: anti-hail nets give the highest certainty equivalents for orchards with **high hail risk and high yield potential**. Insurance suits high-risk, lower-yield orchards; low risk with low yield favours no mitigation. (Per-hectare costs are not available; qualitative use only.)

### 1.5 Sweet cherry, Portugal: net cover vs cracking [PT/EU] [1 trial]

- **Source:** Gonçalves, B. et al. (UTAD and Cermouros). "Orchard Net Covers Improve Resistance to Cherry Cracking Disorder." *Foods* 12(3):543, MDPI, 2023. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9914920/
- **Quote:** "Net cover reduced the natural cracking index by 40%. High fruit weight values were observed in covered trees comparing to the control ones, with increases of 45% and 13%, in 2019 and 2021, respectively." · "with forecasts of worsening heavy precipitation events near harvest, protecting cherry trees with nets will increase res[istance]…"
- Supporting (Norway, plastic rain covers, not nets): marketable fruit 54% uncovered vs 89% covered, 2-year mean (Børve et al., *HortTechnology* 13(1):143, 2003). **[snippet-only]**, so use only if a writer needs it, labelled.

## 3. Banana net houses in Israel [IL]

### 3.1 Screenhouse vs open field, industry level
- Productivity **+30%** (heavier bunches, fewer lost bunches) and **~25% water savings** from net shading. Allows planting in windy plots. Better fruit quality and a longer harvest. Downside: plants about 50 cm taller, so screenhouses up to 6 m high and more labour.
- Israel banana: ~180,000 t from 3,100 ha (2019). 'Grand Naine' selections are ~95% of production.
- **Source:** Elingold, I., Londener, A., Rimmer, A., Tsarfaty, A., Hadar, G., Galpaz, N. (Jordan Valley Banana Research Station, Zemach; MIGAL). "Less Is More? Field Evaluation of Short-Stature Banana Cultivars in a Mediterranean Environment." *Horticulturae* 8(7):619, MDPI, 2022. https://doi.org/10.3390/horticulturae8070619
- **Quote:** "To reduce wind damage, at the beginning of the century the Israeli banana industry shifted cultivation from open fields to screenhouses. Inside the screenhouses, the plants became healthier and more robust, and productivity improved by 30% due to higher bunch weight and a drop in bunch loss. Additional benefits included ca. 25% water savings due to net shading, which significantly reduced evapotranspiration, the ability to grow in plots exposed to high winds that were not suitable for open-field cultivation, improved fruit quality, and prolongation of the harvest period" · "the need to build high (reaching 6 m) and expensive screenhouses" · "ca. 180,000 tons produced in a 3100 ha cultivation area in 2019".

### 3.2 Banana irrigation under 10% and 20% screens (best chart data) [1 trial]
- **Figures (mm/year, average of 2017 and 2018, fully grown plants, Israel):** unscreened 2,330 · 10% Crystal Leno, table-based irrigation 1,814 · 20% Pearl, table-based 1,724 · 10% "dynamic" (previous-day ET0) 1,570 · 20% dynamic 1,368. Yield **was not significantly reduced** (after the first 3 years and first full fruit season). Dust on the screen cut radiation by an extra 10% by the end of the dry season.
- Our arithmetic from these figures (label as derived): −22%, −26%, −33%, −41% vs unscreened.
- Regional rule of thumb from the same abstract: inner valleys about −25%, coast about −10%.
- **Source:** Cohen, S., Hadad, D., Galpaz, N., Israeli, Y., Lukyanov, V., Achiman, O., Londener, A., Elingold, I., Tanny, J. "Exploiting dynamic changes in internal screenhouse climate to inform irrigation in bananas." *Acta Horticulturae* 1268, ISHS, 2020. https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2020.1268.29 · https://ishs.org/ishs-article/1268_29/
- **Quotes:** "In Israel's inner valleys banana irrigation under 10 and 20% screens is reduced below rates for open stands by about 25% while in coastal regions reductions are about 10%." · "While for unscreened plots irrigation averaged 2330 mm year-1 for the two years of fully grown plants (2017 and 2018), below the 10 and 20% screen rates were 1814 and 1724 for non-dynamic and 1570 mm and 1368 for dynamic irrigation, respectively." · "after the first 3 years (and the first full fruit season) yield was not significantly reduced." · "Dust accumulation reduced solar radiation transmission by an additional 10% at the end of the dry season". This supports net cleaning and maintenance.

### 3.3 Jordan Valley review figure
- "In the Jordan Valley water savings can reach 20-30% with the same and even better yield." Main drivers: reduced radiation and wind.
- **Source:** Tanny, J., Cohen, S., Israeli, Y. "Screen constructions: microclimate and water use in Israel." *Acta Horticulturae* 927, ISHS, 2012. https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2012.927.63 · https://ishs.org/ishs-article/927_63/
- **Quote:** "In Israel, much of the banana cultivation has moved inside screenhouses in order to save water and improve fruit quality. In the Jordan Valley water savings can reach 20-30% with the same and even better yield. … the major factors contributing to evapotranspiration reduction under summer conditions are radiation and wind speed reduction."

### 3.4 Typical banana net, wind inside, screen texture
- "Banana screenhouses are currently equipped with transparent shading screens, with a nominal shading of about 8-15%." Knitted vs woven 10% screens: under knitted, air velocity +18% and radiation +7%. Pressure drop on knitted = 56% of woven. Other studies link this to more leaf tearing under knitted screens.
  - Source: Tanny, J., Pirkner, M., Teitel, M., Cohen, S., Shahak, Y., Shapira, O., Israeli, Y. "The effect of screen texture on air flow and radiation transmittance: laboratory and field experiments." *Acta Horticulturae* 1015, ISHS, 2014. https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2014.1015.4
  - Quote: "under the knitted screen, horizontal mean air velocity and global radiation were on average larger by 18 and 7%, respectively, than those measured under the woven one." Message: two nets with the same "10%" label behave differently, so net selection is an engineering decision.
- Wind inside a 15% woven banana screenhouse (352 × 228 × 6 m, northern Israel): u_int = 0.27·u_ext − 0.11 (R² = 0.89). Source: Tanny, J., Cohen, S., Grava, A., Haijun, L. "Airflow and turbulence in a banana screenhouse." *Acta Horticulturae* 719, ISHS, 2006. https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2006.719.72. Quote: "the inside air velocity was significantly lower than outside wind speed, the relation being u int =0.27 u ext – 0.11 (R² = 0.89)". (Our illustration: 5 m/s outside gives about 1.2 m/s inside. Label as computed from the regression.)
- Commercial context (screenhouse sizes): 300 m × 230 m × 6 m flat-roof banana screenhouse in northern Israel (Tanny, Dicken, Cohen, *Acta Hort.* 797, 2008, https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2008.797.9). Quote: "Shading banana and other orchard crops with screens is increasingly popular due to the resulting decreased water use and increased fruit quality."

## 4. Shade nets: temperature, radiation, quality, yield

### 4.1 Avocado, Mediterranean (Spain) [PT/EU] [1 trial]
- Young 'Hass' on 'Duke 7', Callosa d'En Sarrià, Alicante, summer 2022. Net house 6 m high, white PE monofilament, ~76% light transmission. Effects: PPFD max ~2000 → ~1400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ (≈−30%). **Leaf temperature −3 to −5 °C** (open >40 °C at midday, shaded rarely >37 °C). Leaf VPD −20 to −25% (5.5–6.0 → 4.0–4.5 kPa). Air temperature at midday was similar (~35 °C). Mornings and evenings were about 2 °C warmer under the net.
- **Source:** Tasa, M., Badal, E., Bonet, L., Martínez-Gimeno, M.A., Pérez-Pérez, J.G. (IVIA). "Shade Nets Improve Gas Exchange and Chlorophyll Fluorescence in Young Avocado Trees Grown Under Mediterranean Conditions." *Plants* 14(23):3550, MDPI, 2025. https://doi.org/10.3390/plants14233550
- **Quote:** "In summer, open-air leaf temperatures reached values above 40 °C at midday, while shading consistently reduced Tleaf by 3–5 °C, rarely exceeding 37 °C" · "shading reduced incident radiation by ~30%" · "the net-house did not consistently decrease air temperature". This is honest framing: the net cools the leaf, not the air.
- Same paper: FAO global avocado area ≈ 858,152 ha (2021). Quote: "global avocado cultivation reached approximately 858,152 hectares in 2021".

### 4.2 Avocado, South Africa [1 trial per site]
- 20% white or 30% crystal nets, 6.5 m high (Mooketsi, Karkloof). Canopy temperature about **−5 °C in the afternoon**. Air temperature difference <0.5 °C. Wind "eliminated". Solar irradiance −18% / −19%. Packout improved through less sunburn and wind damage. **But yields under the net were lower in 2015 at both sites** (authors cite irrigation and pollination issues).
- **Source:** Blakey, R.J., van Rooyen, Z., Köhne, J.S., Malapana, K.C., Mazhawu, E., Tesfay, S.Z., Savage, M.J. (Westfalia; UKZN). South African Avocado Growers' Association Yearbook 38, 2015, pp. 76–. https://www.avocadosource.com/Journals/SAAGA/SAAGA_2015/SAAGA_2015_38_PG_76.pdf
- **Quotes:** "Shade netting has shown to be very effective in improving the packout … by reducing sunburn and wind damage. The shade netting only has a slight effect on air temperature and relative humidity, but reduced canopy temperature by about 5°C in the afternoon." · "The reduction in yield under the shade net for the 2015 harvest at both sites is a major concern." Use this honestly: success needs agronomy (irrigation, pollination, spacing), which supports the turnkey message.
- Tinyane, van Rooyen, Sivakumar, SAAGA Yearbook 40, 2017 (20% shading, Hass): "Overall, 20% shading remarkably reduced the sun damage and improved the marketable total yield." https://www.avocadosource.com/Journals/SAAGA/SAAGA_2017/SAAGA_2017_40_PG_088.pdf

### 4.3 Photoselective nets, Israeli research (Volcani / Shahak) [IL]
- **Citrus, Israel (5-year study, Valencia orange, red / pearl / transparent top nets):** yield **+18% (transparent), +27% (pearl), +44% (red)** vs un-netted control. WUE was about 23% / 15% / 28% higher under pearl and red at I100 / I80 / I60. A 20% cut in fertigation did not reduce yield.

"Top Photoselective Netting in Combination with Reduced Fertigation Results in Multi-Annual Yield Increase in Valencia Oranges (Citrus sinensis)." *Agronomy* 11(10):2034, MDPI, 2021. https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy11102034
  - Quote: "at all fertigation regimes, the nets resulted in a higher yield compared to the control … the overall fruit mass (yield) on the tree increased by 18%, 27%, and 44% under the transparent, pearl, and red net, respectively." · "increased productivity of orange trees grown under photoselective nets, particularly the red net … was achieved with a considerable water-saving effect."

### 4.4 Winter shade nets for chill hours (Extension Service + MIGAL, 2026) [IL]
- Trial across **30 orchard plots** (Golan to Shfela). **90% black shade nets** over peach, nectarine, apricot, **cherry**, plum and pear through fall and winter. Chill hours **+~24%**; yield **+82% on average** vs uncovered controls; some orchards went from 1 to 2 t/dunam. Cost ₪4,000–5,000/dunam (installed and removed seasonally). Extra income in cherry ₪8,000–15,000/dunam/yr (quoted by the instructor).
- **Source:** Yair Kraus, "The shade war: netting nearly doubles fruit yields in Israeli orchards," Ynetnews, 24 Aug 2026. https://www.ynetnews.com/environment/article/ryirpwkpge. Quoting Itay Bamberger (Ministry of Agriculture Extension Service, deciduous fruit) and Dr. Michal Ackerman-Lebert (MIGAL).
- **Quotes:** "Field data showed an increase of about 24% in accumulated chill hours and an average 82% jump in yields compared with control plots left uncovered. In some orchards, yields even doubled, from one ton to two tons per dunam." · "costs between 4,000 and 5,000 shekels per dunam" · "The gains from using the nets, for example in cherry orchards, amounted to an additional 8,000 to 15,000 shekels a year."
- **Caveat:** this is a news report of extension-trial results, not a peer-reviewed paper. Present it as "ניסוי של שה"מ ומיגל, כפי שדווח". The ₪/dunam figures are industry figures, not MIFTAH prices.

### 4.5 Recommended shading % by crop
- No authoritative Israeli table of "recommended shading % per crop" was found. What the sources show:
  - banana: 8–15% transparent (Tanny et al. 2014). Israeli trials used 10% and 20% (Cohen et al. 2020). MIFTAH's Western Galilee project uses 11%.
  - apple: 12–27% light reduction (Middleton & McWaters 2000); 20% white (Kalcsits 2018).
  - avocado: 20% white / 30% crystal (SA trials); ~24% light reduction (Spain, 76% transmission).
  - pepper (Israel, photoselective): 30–40% (Shahak et al. per secondary sources, **[snippet-only]**).
- Recommendation: describe shading % as crop-, region- and cultivar-specific. Do not publish a "recommended %" table as fact.

## 5. Insect-proof nets

- **Israel, tomatoes and TYLCV:** whitefly transmits TYLCV; "Until 1990 this was exclusively by insecticides." Ten screen materials were tested and three adopted; "by 2000 practically all table tomatoes in Israel were grown under exclusion screens. The use of exclusion screens has been shown to be an economically viable pest management method." Source: Berlinger, M.J., Taylor, R.A.J., Lebiush-Mordechi, S., Shalhevet, S., Spharim, I. (Gilat station; Volcani). "Efficiency of insect exclusion screens for preventing whitefly transmission of tomato yellow leaf curl virus of tomatoes in Israel." *Bulletin of Entomological Research* 92(5):367–373, Cambridge UP, 2002. https://doi.org/10.1079/BER2002180 [IL]
- **Screen trade-off (engineering point):** high-mesh insect screens cut ventilation by **50–70%** and crop water use by **~50%** vs an open pepper field (Tanny, Cohen, Israeli 2012, Acta Hort 927; quote: "Measurements have shown a reduction of about 50-70% in ventilation rate, and about 50% reduction in crop water use, as compared to estimated values for an open pepper field."). In a 50-mesh commercial pepper screenhouse, water use was **~1/3** of open-field potential ET (1.8–2.1 mm/d in Aug 2001; 1.4–1.6 mm/d in Sep 2001): Möller, M., Tanny, J., Cohen, S. et al., *Acta Horticulturae* 659, ISHS, 2004, https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2004.659.74. Quote: "The measured water consumption is about 1/3 of the potential evapotranspiration calculated for a pepper crop grown in the open field." [IL]
- **Orchard exclusion netting [1 trial]:** Minnesota apple, 2021–2022, hail netting (DrapeNet). Codling moth **−94%**, apple maggot **−96%**, red-banded leafroller **−56%** vs open plots. "insecticide application alone did not significantly reduce pest pressure". Netting did not significantly change yield.
  - Source: Nelson, S.G.A., Klodd, A.E. et al. "Hail netting excludes key insect pests and protects from fruit damage in a commercial Minnesota apple orchard." *Journal of Economic Entomology* 116(6):2104–2115, Oxford UP / ESA, 2023. https://doi.org/10.1093/jee/toad197 · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10711545/
  - Quote: "the codling moth … and the apple maggot … were significantly reduced inside the netting compared to open plots by 94% and 96%, respectively. For a secondary pest, the red-banded leafroller …, moth populations were reduced by 56%."
  - Economic companion paper (Frontiers in Insect Science, 2023, doi 10.3389/finsc.2023.1266426): "The PB analysis indicated that the netting system was an economically competitive alternative to conventional insecticide applications." Context: "In Minnesota, conventional apple growers report applying insecticides more than 7 times on average throughout the production season." https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/insect-science/articles/10.3389/finsc.2023.1266426/full

## 9. Structures and engineering (why factory QA matters)

- **Forces on orchard shade nets (Israel, peach net-house 232 × 100 m) [IL] [1 trial]:** 60 load cells on a 40 × 6 m upwind edge section. The force grows as a 2nd-order polynomial of wind speed, and is strongest where the net attaches to perimeter cables. Extrapolated to 150 km/h, the largest point force reaches ~50 kg.
  - Source: Ibanez, P., Teitel, M., Tanny, J. (Volcani). "Effect of wind speed and direction on forces acting on shade nets covering orchard trees." *Acta Horticulturae* 1227, ISHS, 2018. https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2018.1227.20
  - Quotes: "the force acting on the net at the point where the load cell is attached to the net is a second-order polynomial of wind speed. The forces acting on the net were stronger at the points where the net was attached to a cable at the outer edge of the net-house." · "at a wind speed of 150 km h-1, the largest force acting at the point where the load cell was attached to the net could be 50 kg." · "The use of nets and screens to cover orchards has greatly expanded during the last decade".
  - Message: doubling wind speed roughly quadruples the load, and edges and perimeter cables carry the peak. This is why anchoring, cable tensioning and edge detailing are engineered, and why components are tested before they go to the field.
- **Israeli wind-load standard:** ת"י 414 "עומסים במבנים: עומסים כלליים – עומס רוח" (SII). It sets characteristic wind loads for structural design of buildings and civil-engineering works, using the basic wind-speed map of Israel (Annex A). https://www.sii.org.il/he/lobby/standardization/standard-page?id=1550. Quote: "תקן זה מביא הנחיות לקביעת עומסי רוח טבעית עבור תכן מבני של בניינים ועבודות הנדסה אזרחית". **Caveat:** I found no Israeli standard written specifically for net houses. Do not claim "MIFTAH complies with ת"י X" unless the owners confirm it.
- **European standard:** EN 13031-1:2019 (supersedes 2001) "Greenhouses – Design and construction – Part 1: Commercial production greenhouses". It covers mechanical resistance and stability, serviceability and durability, foundations included. Wind actions per Eurocode EN 1991-1-4. BSI: "specifies principles and requirements for the mechanical resistance and stability, serviceability and durability for design and construction of commercial production greenhouse structures, including their foundations, irrespective of the material used". https://shop-checkout.bsigroup.com/products/greenhouses-design-and-construction-commercial-production-greenhouses. **Caveat:** whether net houses fall within the scope of EN 13031-1 was not verified. Phrase it as "the European reference for agricultural structures" without claiming formal applicability.
- **Ageing and dust:** dust cut radiation transmission by an extra 10% by the end of the dry season (Cohen et al. 2020). PAR transmission falls ~2% per year (Blanke 2009 via Mupambi 2018). Both support a maintenance-programme message.
- **Height:** banana screenhouses reach 6 m (Elingold 2022). Tall structures have larger wind exposure (qualitative point).
- **Gaps funnel wind:** USDA AF Note 25 quote in §7. Applies to windbreak and net-wall continuity.
